# [Reducing a four-wire feeder, step by step](@id tutorial-network-reduction)

*Three reductions, three different answers to "what may I forget?"*

You have a detailed four-wire LV model and a study that would run faster on a
smaller one. This tutorial takes one feeder through the three reductions in
BMOPFTools, in the order you would use them in practice, and at each step asks
the same two questions: **what did it remove, and what did that cost?** By the
end you will know when each tool is exact, when it is not, and how to check.

The companion pages explain the theory: [Network reduction](@ref network-reduction)
compares the tools on real feeders, and
[Reducing a feeder with a voltage guarantee](@ref tutorial-opti-kron) explains
how Opti-KRON's guarantee works, using a six-bus example.

*Prerequisites: `BMOPFTools`, `JuMP`, `HiGHS` and `Ipopt`. The complete script is
`examples/opti_kron/reduction_workflow.jl` (about 30 s); every number below is
its output. The feeder is generated by the script, so no external data are
needed.*

## 1. The feeder

```text
mv ──Dyn── lv0 ── p1 ── p2 ── … ── p12        trunk, 40 m spans
                        │           │
                        q1 … q4     r1 … r4   laterals at p4 and p8, 30 m spans
```

An 11 kV / 415 V transformer feeds a four-core cable trunk with two laterals.
Thirty single-phase customers sit on rotating phases, two per loaded pole;
five poles carry no customer. The neutral is solidly earthed at the
transformer and, as is common in practice, through finite MEN electrodes
(10 Ω) at every third pole.

```julia
net = synthetic_lv_feeder()          # defined in the example script
# feeder: 22 buses, 20 lines, 30 customers, 4 MEN electrodes, Ybus 86 nodes
```

For loadings we use two days of hourly data: an evening peak and per-customer
noise, with a third of the customers exporting PV at midday. **Day 1 trains**
the reductions; **day 2 tests** them, so every error below is measured on
loadings the reduction has not seen. The ground truth is an AC power flow of
the full model on day 2 (lowest phase-to-neutral voltage 222.0 V).

## 2. Neutral Kron reduction: is the neutral really at earth?

Eliminating the neutral conductor turns the four-wire model into a three-wire
one. It is a Schur complement, Z_abc = Z_pp − Z_pn Z_nn⁻¹ Z_np, and it is
**exact exactly when the neutral is at earth potential at both ends of every
line**. So the first step is not to reduce, but to check:

```julia
nk = kron_reduce_neutrals(net)
nk["_meta"]["kron_reduction"]["classification"]
# "grounded_neutral_projection", with 20 forced-ground buses
```

The reducer reports that it had to *force* the neutral to earth at 20 buses.
Only the transformer bus was perfectly earthed; the MEN electrodes are 10 Ω,
not 0 Ω, and the poles between them have no electrode at all. On the test day
the cost is plain:

```julia
# neutral Kron: Ybus 86 → 66 nodes, worst voltage error 5.94 V
```

A 25% smaller matrix bought a 6 V error, more than 2% of the voltage, and it
cannot be undone, because the neutral voltages it threw away are exactly the
information that was needed. The scientific contract
[`check_kron_boundary_recovery`](@ref) refuses the same transformation for the
same reason.

!!! tip "Lesson 1"
    Neutral elimination is a statement about earthing, not about cables.
    Apply it only where the model earths the neutral perfectly (check for
    `"classification" => "exact"`); here we keep the four-wire model.

## 3. Simplification: exact, but what does it forget?

[`simplify_network`](@ref) merges lines through poles that carry nothing:
no load, no shunt, one line in, one line out.

```julia
sp = simplify_network(net)
# simplify: 22 → 18 buses, worst error 5.68e-14 V, voltages kept for 17 of 21 LV buses
```

The error is round-off: merging series impedances through an empty bus is
exact. But four poles (p1, p2, p5, q1) no longer exist in the model, so their
voltages are no longer computed. Pole p9 carries no customer either, yet it
survives: its MEN electrode is a shunt that must stay attached somewhere.

!!! tip "Lesson 2"
    Simplification is exact for what it keeps and silent about what it
    removes. Use it when the voltages at pass-through poles do not matter. It
    cannot remove a pole that has a customer.

## 4. Opti-KRON: removing loaded buses, with a guarantee

To remove buses that *do* carry customers, the customers must move. Opti-KRON
moves each eliminated bus's devices to a nearby kept bus (a *super-node*),
uses the super-node's voltage as the estimate, and chooses the fewest
super-nodes such that the estimate stays within Ē of the true voltage. That
guarantee holds under a linear voltage model, for every loading in an
**uncertainty set** built from the training day.

```julia
m = opti_kron_model(net; H=4)                      # candidate super-nodes within 4 hops
U = opti_kron_load_injections(m, net, train)       # 24 training loadings
res = opti_kron_reduction(m, OptiKronBox(U), 0.01; optimizer=HiGHS.Optimizer,
                          keep=opti_kron_required_buses(net))   # keep transformer buses
```

The choice of set is a choice of how much to trust the training day. Each set
was reduced at two thresholds, then checked on the test day:

| Set | Ē = 0.005 p.u. | test-day error | Ē = 0.010 p.u. | test-day error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scenarios (2 extreme hours) | 10 buses | **1.63 Ē** | 5 buses | **1.19 Ē** |
| convex hull of day 1 | 13 buses | **1.11 Ē** | 8 buses | 0.78 Ē |
| box of day 1 | 13 buses | 0.53 Ē | 11 buses | 0.82 Ē |

Read the table from top to bottom:

- **Scenarios** are the classical approach: guarantee the bound at the
  heaviest and lightest hours. They keep the fewest buses but break the bound
  on an ordinary day, because the error depends on *where* the load is, not
  only on how much there is. PV export at a few poles is exactly such a
  pattern.
- **The convex hull** guarantees every mixture of the training hours. That
  covers day 1 completely, but day 2 can still step outside it (1.11 Ē at the
  tighter threshold). A certificate covers its set, and only its set.
- **The box** lets every customer vary independently within its observed
  range. It is wider, keeps more buses, and held on the test day.

!!! tip "Lesson 3"
    A smaller set means a smaller model and a weaker guarantee. Build the set
    from data that represent the conditions you will study, and keep a test
    day aside to find out whether it did.

## 5. Build, solve, lift, verify

The assignment is a decision; [`kron_reduce_buses`](@ref) turns it into a
solvable case. Devices move to their super-nodes, and the buses left without
devices are removed by exact Kron reduction of the multiconductor network.

```julia
red = kron_reduce_buses(net, m, res; Ebar=0.01)
# reduced case: 11 buses, 9 lines (0 equivalents), 4 shunts (1 equivalent), Ybus 42 nodes
# kept buses: lv0, mv, p1 … p9
```

Opti-KRON kept the trunk and folded both laterals and the trunk end into it.
Their customers now sit on the trunk, and the MEN electrode at p12 reappears
as an equivalent shunt at p9: earthing is not lost, it is re-expressed. Solve
the reduced case for each test hour, and compare it with the full model:

```julia
pf  = solve_pf(red_at_hour; optimizer=ipopt)
est = lift_solution(net, red, pf; model=m)     # voltages at every original bus
# Opti-KRON box Ē=0.01 (= 2.4 V): worst AC error on test day 2.13 V, after lift 0.21 V
```

The super-node voltages are within the 2.4 V bound in full AC power flow,
not only in the linear model. [`lift_solution`](@ref) then uses the solved
loading to correct each estimate after the fact, bringing the error down to
0.21 V. The reduced case has half the matrix size, and only 5 of the 15
customer poles remain as buses.

## 6. What each step cost

| Step | Kept | Matrix | Error on test day | What was given up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | 22 buses | 86 nodes | — | — |
| neutral Kron | 22 buses | 66 nodes | 5.94 V | neutral voltages and currents; finite earthing |
| simplify | 18 buses | 70 nodes | ≈ 0 | voltages at 4 pass-through poles |
| Opti-KRON (box, Ē = 0.01) | 11 buses | 42 nodes | 2.13 V (0.21 V lifted) | exact device locations; currents of eliminated lines |

!!! tip "The workflow in four lines"
    1. Check earthing before eliminating neutrals.
    2. Simplify when pass-through voltages don't matter.
    3. Reduce loaded buses with Opti-KRON, using a set built from representative
       data and transformer buses kept.
    4. Verify on loadings the reduction has not seen, and lift the answer back.
