Scaling, initialization and diagnostics

Use this route when two nominally similar solves behave differently, or when constructing a reproducible numerical experiment. The detailed equations and supported scaling domains remain in the OPF reference. This page connects the controls to the evidence you should inspect.

Choose coordinates deliberately

The input network and extracted results use SI units. Solver coordinates are a separate choice. OpfScaling exposes SI, classic per-unit, and custom consistent-base policies; an explicit scaling_policy takes precedence over per_unit and s_base.

TaskWhere to go
Learn voltage/current/power bases and objective unitsUnits, bases and economics, which derives the classic bases by hand on a bundled feeder.
Choose a typed policy or experiment with zone-specific basesCustom and zone-local bases below, then Units and scaling for the qualified transformer/converter domains and rejected combinations.
Inspect the coordinates at an AC or DC locationopf_coordinate_bases. Use the location's bases rather than assuming a universal power base.
Inspect model blocks, scaling and initialization evidenceopf_diagnostic_schema. Check the returned capabilities before relying on semantic-block completeness.
Interpret objective weights or compare different norm encodingsObjective weights, norms and pitfalls.

A scaling change is an experiment in numerical coordinates. Compare the recovered physical quantities and residuals, as well as termination status and solve time. The supported policy does not guarantee better conditioning or an identical local solution from every starting point.

Custom and zone-local bases

The per_unit and s_base keywords select the classic policy: one system power base, with voltage bases seeded at the source and propagated through isolating transformers. The typed OpfScaling boundary makes the same choice explicit and lets you vary it reproducibly. The examples use the bundled 10 kV / 400 V feeder from the units tutorial and run when the documentation is built.

using BMOPFTools, JuMP, Ipopt

net = parse_bmopf(joinpath(pkgdir(BMOPFTools), "test", "data",
                           "profiler_systems", "eu_tn_four_wire.json"))

ctx = build_opf_model(net; scaling_policy = OpfScaling(:classic; power_base = 1e6))
classic = opf_bases(ctx).v_base                    # propagated voltage base per bus
Dict{String, Float64} with 4 entries:
  "lv1" => 230.94
  "lv2" => 230.94
  "lv0" => 230.94
  "mv"  => 5773.5

OpfScaling(:si) solves in SI, and OpfScaling(:classic; power_base = …) is the keyword default spelled out. To change the voltage and power coordinates together, supply a complete, compatible voltage-base map. Halving every propagated voltage base while choosing a 200 kVA power base gives:

custom_bases = Dict(bus => 0.5 * base for (bus, base) in classic)
policy = OpfScaling(
    name = :half_voltage_200kva,
    power_base = 200e3,
    voltage_bases = custom_bases,
)
ctx = build_opf_model(net; scaling_policy = policy)

opf_coordinate_bases(ctx, "mv").current == 200e3 / custom_bases["mv"]
true

This is intentionally a consistent family of coordinates. BMOPFTools checks the line, switch, and transformer voltage-base relationships and derives the current, impedance, and admittance bases from them. Arbitrary independent bases would require coefficients in every voltage–current–power equation and are not represented by this policy. When comparing two policies, first establish that the physical solution and derivatives agree, and only then compare solver trajectories; iteration counts alone are not evidence that the mathematical problems match.

Transformer-local power bases

A power base may also differ between galvanic zones. Pass per-bus power_bases alongside the voltage bases — here 1 MVA on the MV side and the transformer's 400 kVA rating on the LV side:

zone_policy = OpfScaling(
    name = :transformer_local_power,
    voltage_bases = Dict(bus => base for (bus, base) in classic),
    power_bases = Dict(bus => (bus == "mv" ? 1e6 : 400e3) for bus in keys(classic)),
)
ctx = build_opf_model(net; scaling_policy = zone_policy)
evidence = opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).transformer_scaling
(applied = evidence["applied_to_model"],
 identity = evidence["power_product_identity_passed"])
(applied = true, identity = true)

The rules that make such a policy admissible follow from the physics:

  • Lines, closed switches, and galvanically continuous regulators share physical terminal currents, so with $I_B = S_B / V_B$ their buses must keep one power base. A regulator's common bushing or straight-through phase also forces one voltage base: its tap is a winding coefficient, not a coordinate discontinuity.
  • An isolating transformer is the natural boundary where the base may change. Its current-coupling, winding-power, KCL-injection, and rating equations then carry explicit side-base coefficients.

With a zone-local policy the engine derives local $I$, $Z$, and $Y$ bases, stamps the side-base ratios into the transformer current balance and leakage referral, scales device limits and costs on their owning zones, and restores physical powers and losses with the side-local bases. A power-base change is supported across every isolating subtype: single_phase, center_tap, wye_delta, delta_wye, and n_winding. The two galvanically continuous regulator families (single_phase_autotransformer, open_delta_regulator) are excluded by design: a proposal with a voltage-base or power-base jump across one is rejected before the model is built.

opf_bases(ctx).s_base is retained under a zone-local policy as a reference value for compatibility and reporting; it is not a universal local power base. Use opf_coordinate_bases(ctx, bus).power (or the s_base_bus table) when writing a bus-local hook or interpreting a local residual.

How the coefficients enter, by subtype:

DeviceWhere the base ratio appears
wye_delta / delta_wyeA reciprocal pair: delta terminal currents are converted into wye-current coordinates by $S_\Delta / S_Y$, and delta-arm impedance acting on the wye current uses $S_Y / S_\Delta$. The wye-coil nameplate and initialization use the wye-side base.
center_tapThe fixed 5×5 primitive is assembled on the primary power base. Secondary star impedances acquire $S_\text{primary}/S_\text{secondary}$, the secondary shunt the reciprocal, and the three secondary current rows are transformed back to their local current base. The normalized primitive is generally nonsymmetric even though the physical primitive is reciprocal, as expected for an operator whose input and output coordinates use different power bases.
n_windingWinding 1 defines the referred-current and ZB coordinates, so winding $k$ enters ampere-turn balance and every ZB column as $N_k (S_k / S_1) I_k$. The ZB matrix is divided once by the winding-1 impedance base; winding-local shunts, limits, and recovered results keep their owning winding's bases.
AC/DC converterAC bus $b$ uses its local $S_\text{ac}(b)$ while the DC grid uses $S_\text{dc}$, so the lossless converter equation carries $S_\text{ac}(b)/S_\text{dc}$. In droop mode, dc_p_ref and the droop output keep the converter's AC power base; the voltage setpoint and deadband keep the DC voltage base. This covers the native lossless P, V, and droop formulations, not custom or lossy converter builders.

opf_coordinate_bases(ctx, bus) and opf_coordinate_bases(ctx, bus; domain = :dc) expose the voltage, current, and power coordinates to hooks without relying on internal base tables, and opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).acdc_scaling records every stamped converter coefficient and the qualified controller mode. Each of these domains is covered by tests that compare SI and local-base solves on solved state, losses, constraint sets, initialization, and physical Jacobians.

Auditing a proposed base change

opf_diagnostic_schema can also audit a proposal without changing the model. The snippet below is schematic: "hv" and "delta" stand for the buses of a two-zone network of your own, and it does not run on the tutorial feeder.

proposal = opf_diagnostic_schema(
    ctx;
    voltage_bases = Dict("hv" => 6350.0, "delta" => 415 / sqrt(3)),
    power_bases = Dict("hv" => 10e6, "delta" => 1e6),
).transformer_scaling
proposal["proposal_admissible"]
proposal["power_product_identity_passed"]
proposal["requires_new_transformer_stamping"]

The report partitions buses into galvanically continuous zones and gives every transformer interface's voltage-, current-, and power-coordinate ratios. It checks the identity $V_B I_B = S_B$ and records whether explicit current and power conversion is required. For directly galvanic regulator interfaces it also reports galvanic_voltage_base_compatible; a different voltage base makes the proposal inadmissible even if the power base is unchanged. For a proposal that differs from the context, applied_to_model stays false: this call audits a design and never mutates an existing model.

Power-base sensitivity

Keep the power base explicit when comparing solver settings. In issue #429, the default s_base = 1e6 performed well on the measured French MV/LV feeders; reducing it to 1e4 increased iterations from 35 to 97 on the reported 995-bus example. These observations do not establish a universal best base. A base derived from total load or the largest transformer rating has not been validated by that comparison, and BMOPFTools keeps its 1 MVA default.

Per-zone power bases, written OpfScaling(; voltage_bases, power_bases), are an experimental coordinate policy with a qualified device domain. Seeding those bases from transformer ratings is a separate experiment, not an automatic recommendation. Compare physical results under the same initialization policy as well as numerical effort, and do not infer a scaling defect only because a chosen base differs from 1 MVA. The adaptive-barrier comparison holds the base fixed, so vary the power base in separate runs rather than together with a solver-option change.

Semantic blocks and local reference scales

After enforce_kcl!(ctx), opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).semantic_blocks lists the engine's paired coordinates and residuals: rectangular real/imaginary voltage and current pairs, active/reactive power pairs, and their paired KCL, voltage-drop, device-power, transformer, switch, and source equations when present. Each entry is an OpfSemanticBlock holding its ordered members, canonical component labels, physical quantity and unit, model-to-canonical transform, and residual-set contract.

The set contract is derived from the actual JuMP constraints. An equality to the origin is :zero_equality; paired source-voltage or load-power equations with nonzero right-hand sides are :scalar_bounds. The distinction matters: treating every equality pair as a zero residual would make a physically false covariance claim.

Selected declarations also carry a local physical reference scale and its provenance. A load active/reactive residual pair, for example, uses the per-phase nominal apparent-power magnitude when it is available. This is a candidate nondimensionalization reference, not an instruction to mutate the model and not the SI conversion factor. It lets an experiment compare system-wide per-unit bases with device-local references without parsing JuMP names or conflating units with numerical equilibration.

enforce_kcl!(ctx)
blocks = opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).semantic_blocks
load_blocks = filter(b -> b.quantity == :power &&
                          b.reference_physical_scale !== nothing, blocks)
(blocks = length(blocks), with_local_reference = length(load_blocks))
(blocks = 85, with_local_reference = 6)

opf_research_provenance(ctx)["semantic_references"] serializes these declarations, including reference-scale sources. Registration is metadata only: it never changes variables, constraints, bounds, or coefficients.

Inspect the start before tuning the solver

The warm-start initialization uses network-wide source, grounding, conductor-map and winding relations to form a zero-current phasor guess. Its transport diagnostics are available through opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).initialization. They describe the generated start, not feasibility of the loaded network.

The initialization policy is invariant to the model coordinates: voltage levels, phase-angle relationships, and zero neutrals are constructed in the physical network semantics and then represented in the chosen units. A different voltage base therefore changes vr_init/vi_init in model units but not the physical initial state, which is what makes solver behavior comparable across scaling policies.

For multi-voltage networks this is enforced by a network-wide sparse phasor transport solve in per-bus nominal-voltage coordinates. Its rows use the actual conductor maps and ideal winding equations, so a phase-B single-phase lateral remains phase B after a label change, center-tap legs remain anti-phase, and Yd/Dy or WYE/DELTA n-winding phase shifts compose through transformer chains. Inspect the evidence rather than assuming it worked:

initialization = opf_diagnostic_schema(ctx).initialization
(applied = initialization["applied"],
 residual = initialization["maximum_normalized_physics_residual"])
(applied = true, residual = 2.5360026907904962e-12)

initialization["maximum_normalized_residual_by_kind"] breaks the residual down by relation. The transport schema covers WYE, DELTA, center-tap, single-phase, autotransformer, and open-delta relations. Zigzag would need an explicit connection matrix in both the model and initialization layers and is not supported.

Follow that section for the construction and its scope, then Trusting the solver for solution branches and start-dependence. The solver guide connects model coordinates, tolerances and independent validation. Changing backends and changing a formulation are separate experiments.

Keep a numerical experiment interpretable

For repeated coefficient changes, follow Repeated solves before reusing a model. For a reported operating point, capture opf_research_provenance using the experiment-provenance guide. It records scaling and model/parameter fingerprints as well as solver evidence; full-model provenance collection can be expensive, so use study checkpoints.

Finish with independent solution verification. For an additional re-solve comparison, use solution projection. These checks serve different purposes from model-coordinate diagnostics.
