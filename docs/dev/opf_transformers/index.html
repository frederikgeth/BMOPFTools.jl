Transformer equations in the OPF

This page is the equation-level reference for how each transformer subtype is stamped into the optimal power flow. It continues the Constraints section of that page: the variables, sign conventions, earth reference, and per-unit bases are defined there and are used here without restating them.

Read Transformer models first for the data model — which subtypes exist, what each field means, and which approximations are made. Read this page when you need the exact constraint rows: to interpret a dual, to audit a result against another solver, or to extend the formulation.

Model and notation

A transformer is modeled as an ideal ratio between coil voltages, a series leakage impedance carried by the winding currents, and a no-load (core) admittance across one winding. Everything is written as voltage and current equalities, which are linear at a fixed tap.

Notation used throughout this section.

  • A coil voltage $U$ is the voltage across one winding: phase-to-neutral for a wye winding (phase-to-earth if the map has no neutral), line-to-line for a delta winding or a line-to-line single-phase winding.
  • Every transformer current variable $c^x$ is the current flowing from the bus into the transformer at that terminal, so its KCL contribution is $-c^x$ at each terminal and the complex loss of the unit is the sum of $v\,\overline{c^x}$ over all its terminals.
  • The nominal turns ratio of the four isolating two-winding subtypes is $N_0 = V^\text{nom}_\text{fr}/V^\text{nom}_\text{to}$ (fields v_nom_from, v_nom_to, SI volts; v_ref_* is accepted as a legacy alias at ingest), and the operating ratio is $N = N_0\cdot\texttt{tap}$ with a dimensionless multiplier tap (default 1).
  • The two regulator subtypes (single_phase_autotransformer, open_delta_regulator) use an effective ratio $n_\text{eff}$ derived from tap_ratio and regulator_type (see below).

  • \[Y_0 = G_0 + jB_0\]

    (g_no_load, b_no_load, siemens) is the total no-load admittance of the unit.

Where the no-load branch sits. For the isolating subtypes the core branch is connected across the coils of winding 2, the to side, and is driven by that winding's coil voltage. It is not a phase-to-earth shunt at the high-voltage terminals. This follows the OpenDSS convention and is verified against its primitive admittance; the per-subtype placement is tabulated in Magnetizing-shunt placement. The two regulator subtypes place it across the from-side winding instead.

Continuous tap optimization

The tap can be a free continuous decision variable instead of a constant, so the OPF chooses OLTC/regulator settings to reduce losses and hold voltages in band. It follows the implicit free-variable pattern (bounds make it optimizable):

subtypetap fieldfree when
single_phase, center_tap, delta_wye, wye_deltatap (mult. on $N_0$)tap_min < tap_max
single_phase_autotransformertap_ratiotap_ratio_min < tap_ratio_max
open_delta_regulatortap_ratio (per reg.)tap_ratio_min < tap_ratio_max (element-wise)

A free tap adds one variable per tap equal to the effective from→to ratio coefficient ($N$ for single_phase and center_tap, $n_\text{eff}$ otherwise). A free wye_delta/delta_wye tap adds a second variable, the reciprocal $n_\text{eff}^{-1}$, pinned by the bilinear row $n_\text{eff}\,n_\text{eff}^{-1} = 1$ so that the leakage referral stays quadratic instead of rational. Using the ideal-core coupling $N\,I_\text{series} = -I_\text{to}$, the voltage drop stays quadratic in the tap (no cubic), so the existing Ipopt NLP solves it unchanged. For the YY family the from-winding leakage of an OLTC scales with the winding turns ($\propto \texttt{tap}^2$); referred to the to side it is constant ($R' = r_\text{to} + r_\text{fr}/N_0^2$, $X' = x_\text{to} + x_\text{fr}/N_0^2$) and the drop is $v_\text{fr} - N v_\text{to} = -N\,(R'\,I_\text{to} \mp X'\,I_\text{to})$, matching OpenDSS's turns-scaled Yprim; at tap = 1 it is identical to the fixed-tap stamping. (The delta_wye/wye_delta coupled delta-arm carries the same exact $\texttt{tap}^2$ referral — the short-circuit impedance referred to the tapped side scales as $\texttt{tap}^2$, the non-tapped side is held at nominal.) The solved tap is reported in the result dictionary as tap/tap_ratio with a tap_binding flag. See the tap-optimization tutorial.

Zero-impedance (ideal) transformers

Because every subtype is expressed as voltage/current equalities (the IVR impedance form $v_\text{fr} - N v_\text{to} = Z\,I$) rather than a nodal admittance $Y = Z^{-1}$, zero winding resistance and zero leakage reactance are admissible: the constraints degrade to the ideal-transformer relation $v_\text{fr} = N v_\text{to}$ (and, for n_winding, $V_1^r = V_{i+1}^r$ with $\sum_k N_k I_k = 0$) with no inversion and no singularity. This holds for all subtypes and is covered by the "ideal (zero-impedance) transformers" tests. (The separate transformer_yprim/nwinding_yprim admittance export is the one place that genuinely inverts $Z$. An ideal coupling has no admittance form, so that export throws for leakage at or below its cutoff; use ybus_augmented for the bordered representation, or shunt_only=true to inspect the no-load part alone.)

single_phase — per-phase YY, Γ-equivalent

One winding pair per phase $k$. $Z_1 = R_1 + jX_1$ (r/x_series_from, Ω on the from-side base) and $Z_2 = R_2 + jX_2$ (r/x_series_to, Ω on the to-side base) are the two winding leakages; $U_{\text{fr},k}$ and $U_{\text{to},k}$ are the coil voltages.

Ideal core (ampere-turn balance):

\[N\,c^{x}_{x,\text{fr},k} + c^{x}_{x,\text{to},k} = 0 .\]

Voltage. Both leakages are referred to the to side at the nominal ratio, $Z' = Z_2 + Z_1/N_0^2$, and written on the to-side current:

\[U_{\text{fr},k} - N\,U_{\text{to},k} = -N\,Z'\,c^{x}_{x,\text{to},k}\]

(real and imaginary parts as two rows). Eliminating $c_\text{to}$ with the ampere-turn balance shows the from-side equivalent the stamp implies,

\[U_{\text{fr},k} - N\,U_{\text{to},k} = \Bigl[\bigl(\tfrac{N}{N_0}\bigr)^2 Z_1 + N^2 Z_2\Bigr] c^{x}_{x,\text{fr},k},\]

which is the familiar $Z_1 + N^2 Z_2$ at tap = 1. Off nominal, the from winding is the tapped one: its leakage scales with the square of its turns, while $Z'$ seen from the to side stays constant. This matches OpenDSS's turns-scaled primitive and keeps the row quadratic when the tap is a variable.

No-load branch. Each of the $n_c$ winding pairs carries $Y_0/n_c$ across its to coil, so the to-side terminal current is series plus excitation and the from side carries the series current only:

\[I^\text{to,term}_{x,k} = c^{x}_{x,\text{to},k} + \tfrac{Y_0}{n_c}\,U_{\text{to},k}, \qquad I^\text{fr,term}_{x,k} = c^{x}_{x,\text{fr},k}.\]

i_max_to limits $I^\text{to,term}$; i_max_from limits the from current. Optional r/x_neutral_from and r/x_neutral_to add a grounding branch $1/(R_n + jX_n)$ from that side's shared neutral terminal to earth.

When all loss fields are absent or zero the model reduces to the ideal $U_{\text{fr},k} = N\,U_{\text{to},k}$.

center_tap — coupled-coil 3-winding (primitive admittance)

Terminal map: terminal_map_from = [t_ph, t_n] (HV phase, HV neutral), terminal_map_to = [t₁, tₙ, t₂] (leg-1, center-tap neutral, leg-2). $V^\text{ref}_\text{to}$ is the per-leg voltage (e.g. 120 V for a 120-0-120 V unit), so $N = V^\text{ref}_\text{fr}/V^\text{ref}_\text{to} = 60$ for a 7.2 kV / 120 V unit.

The split-phase unit is a genuine 3-winding transformer whose two LV half-windings are tightly coupled on the shared core. Modeling each leg with an independent secondary impedance drop omits that mutual coupling and spreads the two legs apart under load. The OPF therefore imposes the OpenDSS-consistent 5×5 primitive admittance $Y_\text{CT}$ (the same one the Ybus exporter builds; see Transformer primitive admittance) as nodal current injections — element current into each of the five terminals $\mathbf I = Y_\text{CT}\,\mathbf V$ — and pins the per-winding current variables (HV series, leg-1, center, leg-2) to those injections for the i_max limits and loss accounting. $Y_\text{CT}$ is reconstructed from the symmetric star leakage arms $Z_1 = R_1+jX_1$ (HV) and $Z_2 = R_2+jX_2$ (each LV leg), with winding 3 dotted at the center tap (leg-2 voltage span $V_{t_n} - V_{t_2}$). It matches OpenDSS's transformer Yprim to machine precision.

The implied current relations are the ampere-turn

\[N\,c^{r,x}_{x,s} + c^{r,x}_{x,\ell_1} - c^{r,x}_{x,\ell_2} = 0 \quad\text{(and imaginary)}\]

and the center-tap KCL (variable index 2 on to side):

\[c^{r,x}_{x,n} + c^{r,x}_{x,\ell_1} + c^{r,x}_{x,\ell_2} = 0 \quad\text{(and imaginary)}\]

Under zone-local OpfScaling(...; power_bases=...), the ampere-turn equation in model coordinates is N (S_primary/S_secondary) c_s + c_l1 - c_l2 = 0. For the fixed primitive, BMOPFTools assembles a reciprocal intermediate primitive on the primary power base, then transforms the three secondary current rows to the secondary base. The normalized matrix is therefore generally nonsymmetric; transforming it back to dimensional coordinates recovers the reciprocal physical primitive.

The no-load shunt $Y_0$ is folded into $Y_\text{CT}$ across winding 2, the LV leg-1 span $(t_1,t_n)$, undivided, following the OpenDSS convention.

Two equivalent stamps. The admittance stamp needs both star arms to be nonzero (it inverts them) and a fixed ratio (it folds $N$ into the matrix). It is therefore used only when $Z_1 \ne 0$, $Z_2 \ne 0$, and the tap is fixed. A free tap (tap_min < tap_max), or either arm equal to zero, switches to the impedance form of the same model, which is linear in the leakages. With $U_\text{hv} = v_{t_\text{ph}} - v_{t_n}$, leg voltages $u_1 = v_{t_1} - v_{t_n}$ and $u_2 = v_{t_n} - v_{t_2}$, HV current $c_s$, and leg-1 series current $c^\text{ser}_{\ell_1} = c_{\ell_1} - Y_0 u_1$:

\[U_\text{hv} - N u_1 = \bigl(\tfrac{N}{N_0}\bigr)^2 Z_1\,c_s - N Z_2\,c^\text{ser}_{\ell_1}, \qquad U_\text{hv} - N u_2 = \bigl(\tfrac{N}{N_0}\bigr)^2 Z_1\,c_s + N Z_2\,c_{\ell_2},\]

with the ampere-turn balance $N c_s + c^\text{ser}_{\ell_1} - c_{\ell_2} = 0$, the center-tap balance above, and the HV return $c_{x,\text{fr},2} = -c_s$. The shared $Z_1 c_s$ term is what carries the coupling between the two half windings. In the stamped rows $N c_s$ is replaced by $c_{\ell_2} - c^\text{ser}_{\ell_1}$, so they stay quadratic for a variable $N$. At zero leakage both forms give the ideal $U_\text{hv} = N u_1 = N u_2$.

Leakage from OpenDSS XHL/XLT/XHT

For a 3-winding OpenDSS unit, the per-pair leakage values must be star-converted before storing in x_series_from/x_series_to:

x_series_from = (XHL + XHT − XLT) / 2 × Vhv² / (100 · s_rating)
x_series_to   = (XHL + XLT − XHT) / 2 × Vlv² / (100 · s_rating)

Using the 2-winding shortcut (full XHL on the HV side, x_series_to = 0) drops the LV-side leakage and spreads the legs apart under load. PowerIO's BMOPF export carries the correct star split for from_dss; BMOPFTools normalizes the no-load shunt convention.

Wye–delta (Yd) / Delta–wye (Dy)

wye_delta has the wye on the from side, delta_wye the delta on the from side. Each core leg $k$ links one wye coil (phase $k$ to the star point) with one delta coil (between two delta terminals). Nominal voltages are given on the same basis on both sides (line-to-line nameplate values by convention), so the coil ratio carries the $\sqrt3$ of the delta connection:

\[n_\text{eff} = \begin{cases} \sqrt{3}/N & \text{Yd} \\ N\sqrt{3} & \text{Dy} \end{cases} \qquad\text{(delta coil volts per wye coil volt)}\]

Let $U_{\text{wye},k} = v_{\text{wye},t^\phi_k} - v_{\text{wye},n}$ be the wye coil voltage, and write $k^+$, $k^-$ for the next and previous phase, cyclically. The delta coil of leg $k$ spans terminals $(k, k^+)$ for Yd and $(k, k^-)$ for Dy (the backward-delta convention, matching OpenDSS). Call that partner terminal $k'$.

Voltage. The delta coil voltage is the wye coil voltage times the ratio, less one series drop driven by the wye coil current:

\[v_{\text{del},t_k} - v_{\text{del},t_{k'}} = n_\text{eff}\,U_{\text{wye},k} - Z_\text{eff}\,c^{x}_{x,\text{wye},k}.\]

\[Z_\text{eff}\]

collects both winding leakages, $Z^\text{w}$ (wye) and $Z^\text{d}$ (delta), as seen from the delta coil. At nominal tap, with $n_\varphi$ phases,

\[Z_\text{eff} = n_\text{eff}\,Z^\text{w} + \frac{n_\varphi}{n_\text{eff}}\,Z^\text{d}.\]

The factor $n_\varphi$ converts the stored delta-side ohms, which are on the delta bus (line-to-neutral) base, to the delta coil (line-to-line) base. The drop uses the wye current, not the delta terminal current, because the current in a delta coil is the image $c_{\text{wye},k}/n_\text{eff}$ of the wye coil current, whereas the delta terminal variable is the difference of two coil currents.

Off nominal, the from winding is the tapped one and the leakage referred to it scales with $\texttt{tap}^2$. With $n_{\text{eff},0}$ the nominal ratio:

\[Z_\text{eff} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{n_{\text{eff},0}^2\,Z^\text{w} + n_\varphi\,Z^\text{d}}{n_\text{eff}} & \text{Yd (wye tapped)}\\[2ex] n_\text{eff}\Bigl(Z^\text{w} + \dfrac{n_\varphi}{n_{\text{eff},0}^2}\,Z^\text{d}\Bigr) & \text{Dy (delta tapped)} \end{cases}\]

Both reduce to the nominal expression at $n_\text{eff} = n_{\text{eff},0}$.

Current (the transpose of the voltage relation, so the ideal part conserves power). With every current measured into the transformer, a delta terminal collects two coil currents of opposite sense:

\[n_\text{eff}\,c^{x}_{x,\text{del},k} = -\bigl(c^{x}_{x,\text{wye},k} - c^{x}_{x,\text{wye},k''}\bigr), \qquad k'' = k^- \text{ (Yd)},\quad k'' = k^+ \text{ (Dy)}.\]

Note the index: the current row pairs leg $k$ with the other neighbor than the voltage row does. The three delta terminal currents sum to zero identically, so a delta winding passes no zero-sequence current.

Star point. The wye neutral terminal returns the sum of the wye coil currents:

\[c^{x}_{x,\text{wye},n} + \sum_{k} c^{x}_{x,\text{wye},k} = 0 .\]

If the wye map lists no neutral there is no such variable and the star point is solidly grounded (see return paths). Optional r/x_neutral_* on the wye side adds a grounding branch $1/(R_n+jX_n)$ from the wye neutral terminal to earth; on the delta side it is rejected.

No-load branch. $Y_0$ is split equally over the $n_\varphi$ coils of the to winding: a delta of branches across the delta coils for Yd, and phase-to-neutral branches on the wye for Dy.

A legacy combined r_series/x_series is assigned to the wye winding at ingest ($Z^\text{d} = 0$); the builder itself reads only the split fields. When all impedance fields are zero the model collapses to the ideal transform.

With zone-local OpfScaling(...; power_bases=...), the current row becomes n_eff (S_delta/S_wye) c_del = −(c_wye,k − c_wye,k″) in model coordinates. Delta-arm leakage referral carries the reciprocal S_wye/S_delta. This reciprocal pair preserves both the connection incidence and V_base*I_base=S_base; omitting either changes the physical transformer rather than merely scaling it.

single_phase_autotransformer — step voltage regulator

A step voltage regulator is an autotransformer: its series and common windings share a bushing, so the from and to sides are galvanically tied, not isolated. With tap ratio $a$ (tap_ratio, regulated volts per source volt) the effective from→to ratio is

\[n_\text{eff} = \begin{cases} 1/a & \text{Type B (standard SVR, default)} \\ a & \text{Type A} \end{cases}\]

The regulating winding is written like a single_phase pair with $N := n_\text{eff}$. With coil voltages $U_\text{fr}$, $U_\text{to}$ (phase to the winding's reference terminal: the neutral for a line-to-neutral regulator, the second phase for a line-to-line one):

\[U_\text{fr} - n_\text{eff}\,U_\text{to} = Z_1\,c^{x}_{x,\text{fr}} - n_\text{eff}\,Z_2\,c^{x}_{x,\text{to}}, \qquad n_\text{eff}\,c^{x}_{x,\text{fr}} + c^{x}_{x,\text{to}} = 0 ,\]

equivalent to a series impedance $Z_1 + n_\text{eff}^2 Z_2$ on the from current. The leakage is not rescaled with the tap. The no-load branch $Y_0$ sits across the from coil.

The shared bushing. The data model exposes the one physical reference bushing as two terminals, one on each bus. When both sides list a reference terminal, the model bonds them with a zero-impedance tie carrying its own current $c^\text{bond}$:

\[v_{b^\text{fr},q} = v_{b^\text{to},q},\]

with KCL contributions $+(c_\text{fr} + Y_0 U_\text{fr}) - c^\text{bond}$ at the from reference and $+c_\text{to} + c^\text{bond}$ at the to reference. Each coil thus returns at its own reference terminal and the bond carries whatever passes between the two buses on the shared conductor. If only one side lists a reference terminal, both returns close there ($I_\text{ref} + c_\text{fr} + c_\text{to} = 0$ plus the excitation).

A lossless ideal regulator ($Z = Y_0 = 0$) collapses to $U_\text{fr} = n_\text{eff}\,U_\text{to}$, that is $U_\text{to} = a\,U_\text{fr}$ for Type B.

Because the two buses are joined by copper, a regulator does not change the per-unit voltage base (see Units and scaling); nameplate v_nom_* fields on a regulator play no part in its equations.

open_delta_regulator — monolithic open-delta

Two single-phase autotransformer windings connected line-to-line across the phase pairs implied by connection (ABBC/BCAC/CABA); per-regulator taps tap_ratio = [a_1, a_2] give $n_{\text{eff},j}$ as above. For each regulator $j$ spanning from-phase pair $(p, q)$ and the matching to-phase pair:

\[\bigl(v_{b^\text{fr},t_p} - v_{b^\text{fr},t_q}\bigr) - n_{\text{eff},j}\bigl(v_{b^\text{to},t_p} - v_{b^\text{to},t_q}\bigr) = Z_1\,c^{x}_{x,\text{fr},j} - n_{\text{eff},j}\,Z_2\,c^{x}_{x,\text{to},j}\]

\[n_{\text{eff},j}\,c^{r,x}_{x,\text{fr},j} + c^{r,x}_{x,\text{to},j} = 0\]

If tap_ratio has one entry, the second regulator uses the same ratio. KCL injects each regulator's line current at the two phases it spans ($+I$ at one, $-I$ at the other); the per-regulator no-load branch $Y_0$ sits across its from-side line-to-line pair. The phase common to both regulators (B in the ABBC arrangement) is a galvanic straight-through — a zero-impedance wire with its own current variable, enforcing

\[v_{b^\text{fr},t_\text{shared}} = v_{b^\text{to},t_\text{shared}}\]

This is the physically-correct "common neutral" model of Yan et al. (2018): the shared phase passes through unchanged while the two regulated line-to-line voltages are boosted by their taps. Without it the line-to-line voltages are still correct but the per-phase reference floats (the unphysical "unspecified neutral" model). See Transformer primitive admittance for the matching bus-admittance form.

The neutral is not part of the bank. No winding of an open-delta bank touches the neutral, and the model does not connect the neutral terminals of its two buses: only the shared phase is bonded. On a four-wire feeder the neutral conductor physically runs past the bank, and that continuity is a separate, explicit element in the case:

  • a neutral-only line with exact zero impedance, or a closed switch, for an ideal through-connection;
  • a neutral-only line with its real impedance when the jumper resistance matters.

Keeping the path explicit is deliberate. A bond built into the regulator would silently short out a finite-impedance neutral path that the case does model, and every tool reading the case has to agree on connectivity: the OPF, the admittance builders, and the provenance analysis all treat the bank's neutral terminals as unconnected. If no explicit path is given, the downstream neutral is a separate island; provenance reports it as E.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL, and a phase-to-neutral load there has no return path unless that neutral is earthed. Two OpenDSS cases pin the behavior, one with an ideal and one with a 0.5 Ω neutral jumper (pf_open_delta_neutral, pf_open_delta_neutral_jumper; see Validating the OPF engine).

n_winding — general n-winding (ZB model, WYE and/or DELTA)

The general n-winding transformer keeps the explicit per-winding current variables $c^{r,w}_{x,j,k}, c^{i,w}_{x,j,k}$ (one per winding $j$ and phase $k$) — it is the same rectangular IVR style as the other devices, not an admittance model that eliminates currents. Keeping both current and voltage variables (rather than substituting an admittance) is what lets the leakage be parameterized down to zero impedance (a lossless / ideal transformer): the leakage equation below stays well-posed at $ZB = 0$ (it collapses to the ideal ratio $V^r_1 = V^r_{i+1}$) with no division by an impedance.

The leakage is the OpenDSS-style $ZB$ matrix referred to winding 1 (an $(n{-}1)\times(n{-}1)$ impedance, exact for any $n$; see Conversion § n-winding). With winding-local power bases, let $f_j=S_j/S_1$. The referred currents in winding-1 current coordinates are $I^r_{j} = N_j f_j\,c^{w}_{x,j,k}$ ($N_j = V^\text{ref}_j / V^\text{ref}_1$); every $f_j=1$ in SI and ordinary system-base per-unit. With referred coil voltages $V^r_j = U_{j,k}/N_j$, per phase/leg $k$:

\[\sum_{j=1}^{n} N_j f_j\,c^{r,w}_{x,j,k} = 0 \quad\text{(ideal core / ampere-turn; and imaginary)}\]

\[V^r_1 - V^r_{i+1} = -\sum_{j=1}^{n-1} ZB_{i,j}\,I^r_{j+1}, \qquad i = 1,\dots,n-1 \quad\text{(complex; real/imag split)}\]

The per-leg leakage/ampere-turn structure is identical for WYE and DELTA windings — only the coil↔terminal incidence differs. The coil voltage $U_{j,k}$ is phase-to-neutral for a WYE winding and line-to-line (phase $k$ minus its delta partner $k^{\pm}$, selected by the winding's delta_roll) for a DELTA winding, whose $V^\text{ref}$ is its line-to-line coil voltage — so the $\sqrt{3}$ coil-base factor lives entirely in $N_j$, and $V^r_j$ stays consistent (per-unit needs no $\sqrt3$ correction, since the bus base is line-to-neutral). A WYE coil injects $-c^{w}_{x,j,k}$ at its phase and $+\sum_k c^{w}_{x,j,k}$ at its neutral; a DELTA coil injects $-c^{w}_{x,j,k}$ at phase $k$ and $+c^{w}_{x,j,k}$ at its delta partner. Referencing winding 1 folds out the core node, so no internal star-node variable is introduced; the optional no-load shunt sits across winding 2, matching the engine's OpenDSS convention. The constraints are all linear. An n_winding unit has no tap: any tap field on the transformer or a winding is rejected at build. This path is independent of the two-bus transformer code and is validated against OpenDSS's own 3- and 4-winding solves, including delta (Dyn/Dyyn) configurations.
