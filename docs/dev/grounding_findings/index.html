Grounding and neutral findings explained

What each grounding and neutral finding means electrically, and how to tell a modeling error from a legitimate feature of the network.

The findings on this page are the ones people most often ask about, because they concern something a positive-sequence tool never shows: where the neutral is, and what ties the network to earth. For the modeling background, read Ground, neutral and earth return first; this page is about reading the profiler's output. Every block is executed when the documentation is built.

The examples use the regional fixtures in test/data/profiler_systems/, named after the IEC 60364 earthing systems. In a TN system the supply neutral is earthed at the source and customer protective conductors connect back to it; eu_tn_four_wire models this as one perfect ground at the transformer star point. In a TT system each customer has its own electrode, connected to the installation's exposed metalwork but not to the neutral, so the network model looks the same as TN: the neutral is earthed at the source only. The Australian and New Zealand MEN (multiple earthed neutral) practice, a form of TN-C-S, bonds the neutral to earth at every customer installation as well as at the transformer, so the neutral has many earth paths in parallel; au_men_four_wire models them as finite electrodes (10 Ω at the transformer, 30 Ω at each customer). Modeled instead as perfect grounds at every bus, an MEN neutral is held at zero volts everywhere, and I.PROV.KRON_REDUCIBLE then fires for a legitimate reason. An IT system is unearthed or earthed through a high impedance; the it_delta_island_* fixtures cover it.

using BMOPFTools

case(name) = parse_bmopf(joinpath(pkgdir(BMOPFTools), "test", "data",
                                  "profiler_systems", name * ".json"))
codes(report, prefixes...) = sort([f.code for f in report.findings
                                   if any(p -> occursin(p, f.code), prefixes)])

Two different questions

The profiler asks two separate questions, and they are easy to confuse.

  1. Does every island have a voltage reference? An island is a set of buses joined by conductors; transformer windings separate islands. Every voltage in the model is measured to one common reference (earth). If nothing in an island is tied to that reference, its voltages can all shift by the same amount and still satisfy every equation: the problem has no unique answer. This is E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE.
  2. Does every neutral conductor have a path to earth? A neutral section is the set of buses joined by a neutral conductor. If it reaches no earth, the neutral's potential is undefined even when the phases are referenced some other way. This is E.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL (or W. when nothing uses the neutral).

Both questions, and the conductor-group test below, read one body of evidence. What counts as a tie to the reference:

In the dataCounts as
perfectly_grounded_terminals on a busa zero-impedance tie
a voltage_sourcepins each of its terminals, and the neutral of its bus
a shunt row with a nonzero sum (for example a one-terminal earth electrode)a finite tie, for that terminal
a WYE or one-terminal SINGLE_PHASE load with no neutral in its map, on a phase with nonzero demanda tie for that phase terminal: the solver writes the load against earth
a generator written the same way, with both P and Q fixed and not both zerothe same. A generator with a dispatch range is not a reference: a shift of its terminal voltages is absorbed by its output
line shunt admittance to earth (G/B_from/to, the line's capacitance), as the engine stamps it: from the linecode scaled by length, or from the line itself when it carries inline impedance; over the mapped conductors onlya finite tie at both ends
a transformer's r_neutral_* / x_neutral_* on a side with a neutral terminala finite tie at the winding's star point

A delta-connected capacitor bank, whose admittance rows sum to zero, ties phases to each other and not to earth, so it does not count.

A tie references only the conductors it reaches. Conductors form groups through the elements that fix voltage differences between them:

  • a line conductor, end to end;
  • the terminals of one transformer winding;
  • the two ends of a load, generator or IBR coil that can carry current. A zero-power load coil, or a generator coil whose bounds or ratings force zero current, ties nothing; for IBR coils see the note below;
  • two shunt terminals with a nonzero admittance entry between them (listing a terminal in a shunt's map does not by itself connect it to anything);
  • the terminal pairs a capacitor bank actually stamps.

Each group needs its own tie. An earth shunt on a neutral that is carried along a delta feeder, with no winding or load between that neutral and the phases, leaves the phases free to shift together; E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE then names the floating group (detail["scope"] = "conductor_group"), although the island as a whole has a reference.

When an IBR counts as a connection

An IBR coil ties its two terminals unless the constraints the solver stamps on it (P and Q bounds, power-factor rules, zero-base Volt-var or Volt-watt laws, DC-link balance) force its active and reactive power to zero at every voltage; voltage-dependent limits are never used to drop a coil. See E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE in the finding-code reference.

A floating neutral

Start from the European feeder, whose neutral is earthed at the transformer, and remove that earth:

net = case("eu_tn_four_wire")
delete!(net["bus"]["lv0"], "perfectly_grounded_terminals")
report = analyze(net)
println(codes(report, "FLOATING", "NO_VOLTAGE", "WYE_NEUTRAL"))
["E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE", "E.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL", "I.PROV.WYE_NEUTRAL_UNGROUNDED"]

Three findings, one cause. The neutral joins the LV buses but reaches no earth (E.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL); since it was the island's only tie to the reference, the island has none either (E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE); and the INFO names the transformer star point where the earth is missing.

When it is right: always, for a solver. This case has no unique solution.

The repair is one earth, and which kind is a modeling decision. A real electrode is not a perfect ground:

net["shunt"] = Dict{String,Any}("electrode" => Dict{String,Any}(
    "bus" => "lv0", "terminal_map" => ["n"], "G_1_1" => 1 / 10.0, "B_1_1" => 0.0))
report = analyze(net)
println(codes(report, "FLOATING", "NO_VOLTAGE", "WYE_NEUTRAL"))
println(report.results[:provenance]["grounding"]["n_grounding_points"], " earthing point")
String[]
1 earthing point

A 10 Ω electrode clears all three. In this case it is the only earth, so no current can return through it: the star-point neutral sits at earth potential and the neutral rises along the feeder by the drop of the neutral current, the same solution as with a perfect ground. The choice matters once there is a second earth path, such as customer multiple-earthed-neutral (MEN) bonds, distributed electrodes, or a fault: a perfect ground then diverts return current from the neutral conductor into earth that a real electrode would not (see Ground, neutral and earth return). Use perfectly_grounded_terminals only where zero neutral voltage is a fair statement, such as a substation earth mat.

The unused neutral

If a neutral section floats but no load or generator connects to it, nothing depends on its potential, and the severity drops:

net = case("eu_tn_four_wire")
delete!(net["bus"]["lv0"], "perfectly_grounded_terminals")
for l in values(net["load"])           # single-phase loads go phase-to-phase
    l["configuration"] == "SINGLE_PHASE" && (l["terminal_map"] = ["a", "b"])
end
delete!(net["load"], "shop")           # the wye shop uses the neutral: remove it
println(codes(analyze(net), "FLOATING", "NO_VOLTAGE"))
["E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE", "W.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL"]

The neutral finding drops to W.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL: latent rather than active, because no device depends on the neutral's potential. The island finding stays an error, because the island as a whole still has no tie to the reference. The two questions are answered separately, and one earth answers both.

Earthing through an impedance at the transformer

Many MV systems earth the transformer star point through a resistor or an arc-suppression coil, and some LV systems use an impedance too. In the data that is r_neutral_to / x_neutral_to (or _from) on the transformer:

net = case("eu_tn_four_wire")
delete!(net["bus"]["lv0"], "perfectly_grounded_terminals")
net["transformer"]["delta_wye"]["sub"]["r_neutral_to"] = 5.0     # a 5 Ω earthing resistor
report = analyze(net)
println(isempty(errors(report)) ? "no errors" : [f.code for f in errors(report)])
no errors

The star point is tied to earth through 5 Ω, so the island is referenced and the neutral is not floating. The same field on a side that has no neutral terminal (the delta side) is ignored by the network builders, so it is not counted there.

An earthing impedance on the source bus

A voltage_source pins the neutral of its own bus to zero volts. A shunt on that terminal then carries no current, and the profiler says so with W.DOM.SHUNT_ON_GROUNDED. To study an impedance-earthed supply, model the source behind a transformer and put the impedance on the transformer's star point, as above.

An island with no reference

An unearthed delta secondary, or an isolated (unearthed) industrial IT system, has no intentional tie to earth at all. (An IT system may instead be earthed through a high impedance; model that impedance as a shunt.)

bare = analyze(case("it_delta_island_unreferenced"))
finding = only(errors(bare))
println(finding.code)
println(finding.message)
E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE
Galvanic island of 2 bus(es) has no voltage reference (no source, perfect grounding, grounding shunt, line shunt admittance, transformer neutral impedance, or load written against earth) — voltages are defined only up to a shift (rank-deficient): it0, it1.

When it is right: when the model truly contains no path to earth. The line-to-line voltages are defined; the position of the whole triangle relative to earth is not. A power flow on this case returns an answer, and its phase-to-earth voltages are an accident of the starting point.

When the physical system is fine: always, in the sense that such systems operate every day. What ties them to earth is the capacitance of their conductors. Put that in the data and the island is referenced:

capacitive = case("it_delta_island_capacitive")
println("B to earth per meter: ", capacitive["linecode"]["cable"]["B_from_1_1"] * 2, " S/m")
println(isempty(errors(analyze(capacitive))) ? "no errors" : "errors")
B to earth per meter: 1.0e-7 S/m
no errors

The finding is therefore not "this network is wrong" but "this model is missing the thing that references it". If you do not have capacitance data, a high-resistance shunt on each phase at one bus is the usual stand-in; state it as an assumption.

Was the neutral removed, or was it never there?

Most LV networks are four-wire. Many datasets, though, arrive with three conductors because an earlier tool eliminated the neutral (Kron reduction), which assumes the neutral sits at zero volts at every bus. Three findings concern this.

CodeWhat it says
I.PROV.KRON_LIKELYAn LV level has branches but none carries a neutral: the neutral may have been eliminated. A candidate, not a verdict.
I.PROV.KRON_REDUCIBLEEvery neutral is perfectly grounded, so eliminating it would be exact. The explicit neutral adds variables and no information.
W.PROV.IMPLICIT_GROUNDINGComponents reference a neutral terminal, no branch carries a neutral, and some of those neutrals have no grounding: the intended return path is not determined by the data.

A candidate that deserves a look. Take the four-wire feeder and strip the neutral conductor from its lines, as a reduction would:

net = case("eu_tn_four_wire")
for line in values(net["line"])
    line["terminal_map_from"] = ["a", "b", "c"]; line["terminal_map_to"] = ["a", "b", "c"]
end
lc = net["linecode"]["cable"]
net["linecode"]["cable"] = merge(Dict{String,Any}(k => v for (k, v) in lc if !occursin("4", k)),
                                 Dict{String,Any}("i_max" => fill(250.0, 3)))
report = analyze(net)
println(codes(report, "KRON", "IMPLICIT"))
["I.PROV.KRON_LIKELY", "W.PROV.IMPLICIT_GROUNDING"]

The loads still connect to a neutral terminal that no conductor carries: I.PROV.KRON_LIKELY raises the question and W.PROV.IMPLICIT_GROUNDING states the consequence. Go back to the source data: if the neutral was eliminated, the study cannot show neutral voltage rise or neutral current, and you should say so or restore the conductor.

The same absence, harmless. A network that is three-wire by construction raises nothing. The delta island behind a wye-delta transformer has no neutral that could have been removed:

report = analyze(case("it_delta_island_capacitive"))
println(codes(report, "KRON", "IMPLICIT"))
println(report.results[:provenance]["wires_by_level"]["LV_231V"]["delta_fed"])
String[]
true

A level supplied by a delta secondary is recognized as three-wire and not flagged. The check considers only levels below 1 kV and never flags an MV level: three-wire MV is common in some regions (much of Europe and Australia) and four-wire multigrounded MV in others (North America), so a missing MV neutral is no evidence of a reduction.

The explicit neutral that adds nothing

The MEN fixture earths its neutral through finite electrodes, and raises no reduction finding. Replace each electrode by a perfect ground, a common idealization of MEN bonds:

println(codes(analyze(case("au_men_four_wire")), "KRON"))
net = case("au_men_four_wire")
for s in values(net["shunt"])
    net["bus"][s["bus"]]["perfectly_grounded_terminals"] = ["n"]
end
delete!(net, "shunt")
println(codes(analyze(net), "KRON"))
String[]
["I.PROV.KRON_REDUCIBLE"]

With every neutral pinned to zero volts, the neutral conductor carries no voltage information: I.PROV.KRON_REDUCIBLE. For an MEN network that is a consequence of the modeling choice, not a defect; it says the four-wire model can be reduced to three wires exactly. It is a warning sign only where the perfect grounds were added to make a case solve. Finite electrodes keep the neutral voltage rise the perfect grounds remove. See Network reduction for removing such a neutral exactly.

Reading order for a grounding problem

  1. Look at results[:provenance]["grounding"]: n_neutral_components, n_grounding_points, n_floating, and, when neutral conductors are present on branches, floating_components (the bus lists of the floating neutral sections).
  2. If there is an E. finding, find the island or neutral section it names and ask what should tie it to earth. The I.PROV.WYE_NEUTRAL_UNGROUNDED finding usually points at the place.
  3. Decide what kind of tie is true: a finite electrode, a transformer impedance, line capacitance, or a perfect ground. Each is a statement about the physical system.
  4. Re-run and compare codes.

A worked example of the whole loop is in Profile your first messy case.

Related codes

These codes also concern neutrals and earth returns; the finding-code reference has the full triggers.

CodeWhat it says
I.PROV.SWER_ZONEA transformer-isolated zone carries one phase conductor and no neutral on its branches: a single-wire earth return (SWER) section.
W.INT.WYE_WITHOUT_NEUTRALA wye load or generator sits at a bus with no identifiable neutral, which implies an undeclared earth return.
W.INT.IMAX_NO_NEUTRALA star generator or IBR with two or more phases rates only its phase conductors; the neutral, which can carry more current under unbalance compensation, needs its own i_max entry.
