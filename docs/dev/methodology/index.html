How network diagnostics work

This page explains the physics and linear algebra behind three groups of profiler checks: provenance (what an impedance matrix, a default value or a transformer pattern reveals about where the data came from), integrity (grounding, earthing zones and voltage references) and benchmark readiness (whether a case poses a well-defined OPF problem). Read it alongside Analysis & reports when a finding needs more than its message. Solver coordinates and starting values are covered in Scaling, initialization and diagnostics. Numbered citations refer to the Bibliography.

Impedance matrix invariants vs fingerprints

The central design principle: report a violated invariant — a property no legitimate transformation of the data can break — as a data defect (an error or a warning), and treat typical properties as soft provenance fingerprints.

For the series impedance Z = R + jX of a multiconductor line in wire coordinates [1, 5]:

  • Reciprocity (Z symmetric) holds for ordinary passive lines in reciprocal media (isotropic conductors and earth — no gyrotropic or otherwise non-reciprocal materials) — hard (E.PROV.NONRECIPROCAL).
  • Passivity (R ⪰ 0) holds by the dissipation argument, and survives Kron reduction: elimination of grounded conductors is a Schur complement, and Schur complements of accretive matrices remain accretive [10, 11] — hard (E.PROV.NONPASSIVE).
  • Inductive realizability (X ⪰ 0, positive diagonals): the inductance matrix is PSD by the magnetic-energy argument; matrices with both R, X PSD are sectorial, and sectorial matrices are closed under Schur complementation — so X-PSD is also Kron-invariant (W.PROV.X_NOT_PSD, W.PROV.X_NONINDUCTIVE).
  • Mutual resistance sign: Carson's earth-return correction [4] makes off-diagonal resistance positive (≈ ω·μ₀/8 = π·f·μ₀/4 ≈ 0.049 Ω/km at 50 Hz), and nearly equal across conductor pairs — a useful fingerprint of geometry-derived data, but only typical: reductions and fitting can perturb it (I.PROV.NEGATIVE_MUTUAL_R, soft).

Severity follows the consequence of a violated invariant. Asymmetry and a resistance matrix that is not PSD are errors: the first breaks reciprocity, the second lets the line deliver active power. The reactance and susceptance checks (W.PROV.X_NOT_PSD, W.PROV.X_NONINDUCTIVE, W.PROV.B_SIGN below) are warnings. For a symmetric Z the absorbed active power is Iᴴ R I whatever X is (likewise Vᴴ G V for a shunt, whatever B is), so a violation there makes the data physically unrealizable without creating an active-power source.

Sequence-derived matrices and Z₁/Z₀ recovery

A line parameterized from sequence values and transformed back to phase coordinates (TF spec [1], Appendix A.2 / Eq. 148) is perfectly balanced:

Z = Zₛ·I + Zₘ·(J − I),   Zₛ = (Z₀ + 2Z₁)/3,   Zₘ = (Z₀ − Z₁)/3

Geometric (Carson) parameterizations of generic asymmetric untransposed geometries do not produce this, because their phase-pair spacings differ. (Symmetric untransposed arrangements can also be balanced.) The classifier inverts the construction — Z₁ = Zₛ − Zₘ, Z₀ = Zₛ + 2Zₘ — and sorts each linecode into a tier:

tierstructurereading
decouplednear-diagonal phase series blockcoupling/self spread below 10⁻⁶; other factors unassessed
exactly_balancedrtol ≤ 10⁻⁹suggests sequence parameterization or a symmetry assumption; physical symmetry is an alternative
near_balancedrtol ≤ 10⁻²approximate balance signature; review conductor symmetry and modeling assumptions
distinctotherwisenonuniform structure; consider geometry and any prior neutral elimination
unassessedmissing/incompatible X blockfull complex-matrix signature unassessed

I.PROV.SEQ_DERIVED reports exactly_balanced linecodes with their implied Z₁ and Z₀. If the sequence reading is right, phase-pair asymmetry is absent from the inspected block, although retained neutral coupling and other equipment can still create unbalance. Check the source data and whether the intended network shares the symmetry assumption.

Impedance provenance hints

Linecode matrices carry clues to construction choices. Their engineering consequences depend on which conductors and couplings the model retains:

PatternPossible originEngineering implications
negligible phase-block coupling (I.PROV.DECOUPLED_PHASES)per-phase simplificationuniform self terms give approximately Z₀ = Z₁; unequal self terms can still couple sequence networks. Diagonal structure does not exclude prior reduction
three-wire, no PN pattern (I.PROV.IMPEDANCE_TRANSFORM_KR)prior neutral elimination (weak hint)a zero-neutral-voltage model cannot predict neutral rise when inappropriate; total dissipation can remain represented, while separate neutral attribution needs recovery data
uniform R, mutual ≈ ½ self (…_PN)phase-to-neutral equivalentexact under the single-grounded/no-earth-current assumptions; similar conductor resistances explain the pattern, while accuracy depends on earth-current assumptions
uniform R and X (…_MPN)modified phase-to-neutral equivalentreview PN earth-current assumptions and the additional reactance-symmetrization approximation

The equal-self, equal-mutual pattern (I.PROV.SEQ_DERIVED) is the exactly_balanced tier above.

Extra conductors. A conductor outside the inspected phase block can coexist with a sequence-built phase block: OpenDSS can materialize a coupled four-conductor matrix from sequence values, or a modeler can supply neutral parameters separately. Shape alone does not select transposition over sequence parameterization, and a balanced phase block can still have an explicit neutral that introduces phase-to-neutral unbalance. An extra conductor with negligible coupling to the phases is flagged as a possible placeholder or simplified neutral.

Diagonal phase blocks. The per-phase simplification hint also covers unequal diagonal terms, which may couple the sequence networks. A Schur reduction subtracts its correction from the original mutuals and can cancel them, so diagonal data retain an unresolved origin: possible prior reduction is listed as an alternative, and the hint does not declare Kron history impossible.

The PN transformation. Its exact reference domain has no conductor-to-earth shunts, no device current injection into earth, and one grounding point per continuous neutral section. Neutral current and voltage can be recovered from source information in that domain. Multiple earth-return paths can invalidate its assumptions; grounding impedance alone does not determine the error. An R mutual/self ratio near one half is a fingerprint of similar phase and neutral resistances, while the transformation itself also applies to unequal conductors.

The Kron hint is matrix-only, so its context separates known LV use without a represented neutral, known MV/HV-only use, unused templates, and unassessed or mixed use. Missing nominal-voltage or terminal-map evidence does not establish MV/HV use, and unassessed uses keep their conditional neutral implications. Exactly balanced candidates cross-reference I.PROV.SEQ_DERIVED. I.PROV.KRON_LIKELY is the network-level counterpart, for an LV level without a neutral carried by any branch.

Converter-generated source equivalents are classified but never flagged. Scientific checks for reduction and positive-sequence applicability keep their own documented domains; these hints do not widen them. Each hint carries review guidance (reading a hint); its evidence keys are listed under Impedance hint evidence.

The Maxwell sign pattern

Shunt capacitance in wire coordinates is the Maxwell capacitance matrix C = P⁻¹ (P the potential-coefficient matrix from geometry): positive diagonals, non-positive off-diagonals (coefficients of electrostatic induction), nonnegative row sums (capacitance to ground), symmetric PSD — in fact a Stieltjes matrix [5]. OpenDSS Cmatrix is exactly this form; B = ωC inherits it.

The classic data error is entering the pairwise capacitance cij = −Cij with the wrong sign. Severity calibration:

  • PSD / nonnegative diagonals are invariant, so violations are warnings (W.PROV.B_SIGN).
  • The sign pattern is invariant under every exact reduction. Grounding a conductor (a screen bonded to earth, V = 0) keeps a principal submatrix of C; eliminating a floating conductor (zero net charge) takes a Schur complement of C; bundling conductors at equal potential is the congruence SᵀCS with a 0/1 aggregation matrix S; transposition averages permuted copies. Principal submatrices, congruences by S and convex combinations keep off-diagonals ≤ 0 directly, and Schur complements of Stieltjes matrices are Stieltjes [11]. A check on 400 randomly placed conductor groups, computed with an accurate (charge-simulation) solver and put through about a thousand random grounding, floating, bundling and transposition steps, found no off-diagonal above zero.
  • A positive mutual with PSD intact (I.PROV.B_OFFDIAG, informational) therefore comes from how the matrix was computed or entered, not from a reduction. The common cause is the thin-wire potential-coefficient formula $P_{ii} = \ln(2h_i/r_i)/(2\pi\varepsilon)$ applied to thick conductors at close spacing, which ignores the redistribution of charge around each conductor's surface. For four touching insulated cores (an aerial bundled cable or a four-core underground cable) it puts a small positive mutual between diagonally opposite cores, where an accurate solution gives a small negative one (about +2.5 pF/m against −3.4 pF/m for 95 mm² aerial bundled cores). LineConstants-style tools, including overhead_line_constants, use this formula, so matrices for tightly bundled conductors carry the finding by construction; the error is in the opposite-core mutual, and the matrix stays passive. Sequence data with C₀ > C₁ (the mutual is (C₀ − C₁)/3) and a sign-convention mix-up give the same pattern.

Kron reduction, grounding and earthing zones

Two Kron reductions

On this page Kron reduction means eliminating a grounded conductor (usually the neutral) from a line's impedance matrix (kron_reduce_neutrals). Eliminating whole buses, as in error-bounded network reduction (kron_reduce_buses), is the same Schur complement applied to the nodal admittance matrix, under a different condition: zero current injection at the eliminated nodes, rather than zero voltage drop along the neutral. See Network reduction.

A 4-wire network whose every neutral is solidly grounded admits exact Kron reduction of the neutral; conversely, sparse neutral grounding makes the explicit 4-wire model essential [6, 7]. The checks formalize this:

  • wires per voltage level: absent represented LV neutrals are reported descriptively. A possible reduction history needs authored source evidence; wire count alone does not establish it.
  • neutral continuity graph (lines + closed switches carrying the neutral; transformer windings do not pass it): every section must reach a grounding — perfect grounding, a grounding shunt, or a source that pins the neutral. Floating sections leave the zero-sequence path undefined.
  • earthing zones: per galvanic island (transformer windings are galvanic separations) the star-point earthing and the count of downstream neutral electrodes classify the likely earthing system. Downstream neutral electrodes can only exist in multi-earthed systems (TN-C-S/PME/MEN); a source-earthed-only 4-wire zone is genuinely ambiguous between TN-S and TT, because the protective-earth side of installations is not representable in a power-flow data model — the tag states the ambiguity rather than guessing. MV zones use MV vocabulary (solidly/impedance-earthed/isolated).

Voltage reference per galvanic island

Each galvanically isolated island needs at least one voltage reference or its voltages are defined only up to a shift — the nodal admittance matrix is rank-deficient. This is the IEEE 123-bus "bus 610" defect that OpenDSS silently patches with a phantom shunt [2, 12]. A shunt anchors an island only if its admittance has nonzero row sums (Y·1 ≠ 0): a pure delta capacitor bank does not.

OpenDSS default fingerprints

When a .dss file omits a property, OpenDSS substitutes a documented default [9]; after conversion these are indistinguishable from deliberate data except by value. The fingerprint table:

The length check distinguishes scattered exact-1.0 values (default leak) from universal ones (a deliberate length-normalized convention, reported in the convention statement instead) — the adapted ENWL library [8] is the canonical universal case, and its single 1 m source jumper per feeder is the canonical scattered true-positive.

Limit of the method: defaults that the importer resolves at parse time (e.g. basefreq mismatches) leave no trace in the converted data and cannot be fingerprinted downstream.

Regulators and autotransformers

OpenDSS has no first-class regulator branch; modelers encode them either as a near-1:1 wye transformer plus a RegControl (which does not convert), or — per the EPRI autotransformer guidance [9] — as two windings on the same bus (common + ~10 % series winding), kVA rated at the series winding, with a negligible-impedance jumper. Both freeze a control device into a fixed branch; the second bundles three benchmark pitfalls of [2] in one pattern (self-loop topology, deliberate tiny impedance, a rating that reads as 10× overload). Detection (W.PROV.REGULATOR_PATTERN): the self-loop is near-conclusive on its own; the 1:1 form requires corroboration (same-voltage-level endpoints, xpu < 0.5 %, or a non-unity tap), a winding-voltage ratio `vnomto / vnomfrombetween 0.85 and 1.176, and thesinglephasesubtype. The delta-coupledwyedeltaanddeltawye` subtypes are never matched, since regulators are never delta-coupled, which keeps genuine 1:1 phase-shifting interconnectors silent.

This heuristic concerns imported data where a regulator was hacked into a transformer + shunt. The data model itself has dedicated regulator objects — single_phase_autotransformer (a fixed-tap step voltage regulator with the autotransformer shared-neutral coupling) and open_delta_regulator (monolithic open-delta with the galvanic straight-through of the shared phase) — so a regulator authored natively needs no such pattern-matching. See the OPF reference for their constraints and the conventions for the field set. The from_dss converter does not emit these subtypes: regulators arriving from OpenDSS are detected via the pattern above rather than converted.

Benchmark readiness

Raw utility-derived feeders are power-flow cases, not OPF benchmarks: they ship without costed generation (degenerate objective), without voltage bounds, sometimes without thermal limits [2, 3, 14]. The transmission community closed the analogous gap with curated, well-posed benchmark libraries such as PGLib-OPF [13]; the readiness check exists to bring the same discipline to distribution cases. The readiness check encodes the augmentation recipe of the PSCC study [3] — explicit slack generation (loss-minimization objective), dispatchable DERs with diverse costs (I.INT.UNIFORM_GEN_COST screens uniform costs without establishing degeneracy), voltage envelopes including the sequence/phase-to-neutral bounds whose presence measurably improves NLP robustness [3], and cross-section-derived current ratings.

Beyond the structural augmentation check (I.BENCH.AUGMENTATION), four per-network screens describe features worth inspecting after augmentation:

  • W.BENCH.GEN_NO_DOF — generators with p_min ≈ p_max on every phase have fixed active power; reactive dispatch may still be controllable.
  • W.BENCH.GEN_ZERO_COST — dispatchable generators with a zero cost vector have no direct active-generation cost. Network losses, upstream costs and constraints can still determine their dispatch.
  • W.BENCH.GEN_DEGENERATE_COST — pairs of dispatchable generators on the same bus or one hop apart with equal representative costs warrant checking for interchangeable controls. The screen does not establish non-uniqueness.
  • I.BENCH.LOAD_ZERO_PNOM — loads with zero real-power setpoint are zero in active power; reactive demand may remain. Inspect both P and Q.
