Exact substitutions and formulation boundaries

An alternative encoding can remove an auxiliary variable or equation without introducing a modelling approximation within its supported domain. That is the useful meaning of “at no cost” here. It does not promise the same sparsity, conditioning, solve time, constraint multipliers, or derivative behavior at a singular boundary.

Read this after First case and the buses and terminals primer. It explains how to recognize three implementation choices; the OPF model remains the detailed reference for device equations, keys and units.

Substitute, retain, or approximate?

SituationCurrent package behaviorBoundary to preserve
A shunt current is determined by its terminal voltagesInsert the current expression directly into KCL.Keep the full admittance and terminal map; removing an auxiliary variable does not remove the device.
Two orientations describe the same series currentKeep one independent series current and expose the reverse orientation as its negative.Total line-end currents also include their local shunts; they are generally not negatives of each other. Both end limits remain relevant.
A supported load is structurally pure impedanceUse its current law directly, without a squared-voltage auxiliary.The model classification must remain valid over the parameter family; a coefficient whose current value is zero is not necessarily absent.
A transformer leakage inverse is unavailable or below the configured thresholdRetain voltage/current port equations in the augmented admittance builder.Preserve finite leakage; a small value does not become an ideal zero-leakage model.
A nonlinear load is replaced by its admittance at v0ybus_linearized(...; fold=:all, v0) matches the operating-point current.This is an operating-point equivalent, generally not an exact substitution over changing voltages.

The last two rows are explained in nodal admittance builders. Keeping a branch current and its constitutive equation is the classical modified nodal analysis approach when eliminating that current is unsuitable [1].

The zero-voltage example

For a pure-impedance load, the native current law is

\[I=\overline{S_{\mathrm{nom}}}\,\Delta V/V_{\mathrm{nom}}^2, \qquad V_{\mathrm{nom}}>0.\]

It gives zero current at zero coil voltage. If instead we start from only the power relation $\Delta V\,\overline I = S_{\mathrm{nom}}|\Delta V|^2/V_{\mathrm{nom}}^2$, cancellation is valid only when $\Delta V\ne0$. At zero voltage that power equation is $0=0$ and leaves the current unconstrained. The current law supplies the impedance model's physical boundary behavior; it is not equivalent at zero to that weaker power equation alone.

This matters beyond loads: Claeys, Deconinck and Geth [2, §IV-D, eq. (30)] show how replacing KCL by power balance can admit nonphysical solutions at zero nodal voltage in four-wire formulations. A numerical epsilon or an arbitrary lower bound on every neutral voltage is not a substitute for the correct current equations.

BMOPFTools uses the direct law for constant_impedance, pure-Z ZIP, and exponential loads with both exponents equal to two. Mixed ZIP terms are not separately substituted. The load formulation documents the precise routing, retained public current handles, replacement constraint keys, and positive-voltage treatment for other load laws.

Preserve the parameter family and the observables

A parameterized coefficient that currently evaluates to zero must remain in the expression if later updates may make it nonzero. The native load providers preserve that symbolic dependence. Removing the term based on today's value can change tomorrow's model and its sensitivity. See parameterized extensions, including its parameter-update workflow, for supported paths and re-solve checks.

When using or extending a substitution, establish the supported domain, the recovery map for any removed variable, and which physical outputs must agree. Then compare terminal currents, KCL, device limits and power in those units. An affine expression can remain a public current handle even though it is no longer an independent JuMP variable. Likewise, deleting or rescaling a constraint changes how its multiplier must be interpreted; a matching primal voltage alone does not establish matching duals or local sensitivities.

The detailed line, shunt, and load sections own the implementation details. The package's test/opf_device_substitution_tests.jl supplies analytic device witnesses, including zero voltage and parameter updates through zero. The admittance guide explains transformer references and port boundaries. These finite witnesses do not establish a universal formulation-equivalence claim; use independent solution verification for the actual study case.
