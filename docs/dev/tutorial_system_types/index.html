System types and regional practice

What the profiler reports for the wiring and earthing practices used in North America, Europe, Japan and Australia, shown on one small case each, and where its knowledge stops.

Distribution networks are not built the same way everywhere. A European LV feeder is three-phase four-wire with the neutral earthed at the substation. A North American one runs a multi-grounded neutral along the primary and feeds houses from center-tapped single-phase transformers. Japan uses a three-wire 6.6 kV primary and V-connected transformer pairs. Rural Australia uses a single wire and the earth. The profiler has to describe each of these without mistaking a legitimate practice for a defect.

Every case on this page is a bundled fixture in test/data/profiler_systems, and every block is executed when the documentation is built.

Prerequisites

BMOPFTools, plus JuMP and Ipopt for the two power-flow cross-checks.

using BMOPFTools

case(name) = parse_bmopf(joinpath(pkgdir(BMOPFTools), "test", "data",
                                  "profiler_systems", name * ".json"))

function summarize(net)
    report = analyze(net)
    conn = report.results[:connectivity]
    levels = report.results[:voltage_levels]["bus_voltage_map"]
    println(length(errors(report)), " errors, ", length(warnings(report)), " warnings")
    for z in sort(conn["zones"], by = z -> z["label"])
        println("  zone at ", rpad(z["label"], 6), rpad(z["topology"], 13),
                "phases ", z["phases"])
    end
    println("  bus levels (V): ",
            join(["$b=$(round(Int, levels[b]))" for b in sort(collect(keys(levels)))], "  "))
    report
end

summarize prints the three things to look at first for any case: the error and warning counts, the zones, and the bus levels.

A zone is a set of buses joined by lines and closed switches. Transformers separate zones, because their windings are galvanically isolated. Each zone is classified by the phase conductors it carries. A bus level is the nominal voltage of one conductor to the reference, propagated from the source through the transformer ratios.

Europe: three-phase four-wire, earthed at the substation

report = summarize(case("eu_tn_four_wire"))
0 errors, 0 warnings
  zone at lv0   three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  zone at mv    three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  bus levels (V): lv0=231  lv1=231  lv2=231  mv=5774

Two three-phase zones (MV and LV) and an LV level of 231 V, which is 400 V line-to-line. The neutral is earthed once, at the transformer:

g = report.results[:provenance]["grounding"]
println("neutral sections: ", g["n_neutral_components"],
        ", earthing points: ", g["n_grounding_points"],
        ", floating: ", g["n_floating"])
neutral sections: 1, earthing points: 1, floating: 0

One earthing point is enough for the profiler: the neutral section has a path to earth. Whether the installation is TN-S, TN-C-S or TT is a question about the customer's protective earth, which this data model does not represent, so the earthing report says so rather than guessing:

for z in report.results[:provenance]["earthing_zones"]
    println(rpad(join(z["buses"], ","), 14), z["wires"], "; ", z["tag"])
end
mv            no branch conductors represented; grounded infeed reference; protective-earthing system indeterminate
lv0,lv1,lv2   4-wire; grounded infeed reference; protective-earthing system indeterminate

Australia: multiple earthed neutral

The Australian MEN system earths the neutral at the transformer and again at every customer installation.

report = summarize(case("au_men_four_wire"))
g = report.results[:provenance]["grounding"]
println("  earthing points: ", g["n_grounding_points"])
0 errors, 0 warnings
  zone at lv0   three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  zone at mv    three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  bus levels (V): lv0=240  lv1=240  lv2=240  mv=6351
  earthing points: 3

The earths are finite resistances (10 Ω at the transformer, 30 Ω at each installation), modeled as shunt objects on the neutral terminal. The profiler keeps them finite: a 30 Ω electrode is not treated as a perfect ground. For Australian rural networks, the SWER case study covers the single-wire earth-return system; the profiler tags its backbone as a swer zone because the spans carry one conductor and no neutral.

North America: multi-grounded neutral and split-phase service

A 12.47 kV four-wire primary with the neutral grounded along the line, a single-phase lateral, and a pole transformer with a center-tapped 120/240 V secondary.

report = summarize(case("na_mgn_split_phase"))
0 errors, 0 warnings
  zone at sub   three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  zone at svc   split_phase  phases ["a", "b"]
  bus levels (V): lat=7200  p1=7200  p2=7200  sub=7200  svc=120

Read the zones. The single-phase lateral bus lat does not appear as its own zone: it is joined to the three-phase primary by a line, so it belongs to the primary's zone. Only the service bus svc, behind the pole transformer, is a separate zone, and it is tagged split-phase:

split = only(f for f in report.findings if f.code == "I.PROV.SPLIT_PHASE_ZONE")
println(split.message)
Galvanic zone anchored at bus 'svc' is split-phase (fed by center_tap transformer 'pole'): legs ["a", "b"] are anti-phase about the center-tap neutral.

The two legs are 180° apart, so the level of svc is 120 V per leg and a load between the legs sees 240 V. Phase-balance checks that assume 120° separation skip a split-phase zone.

North America: high-leg delta from an open-wye/open-delta bank

Small commercial services often take three-phase power from two single-phase transformers: a center-tapped lighting unit for the 120 V loads and a power unit that closes an open delta. The third phase conductor, the high leg, sits at 208 V to neutral.

net = case("na_high_leg_open_delta")
report = summarize(net)
0 errors, 0 warnings
  zone at sub   three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  zone at svc   split_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  bus levels (V): pole=7200  sub=7200  svc=120

No errors and no warnings. Three things had to be understood for that:

conn = report.results[:connectivity]
vl = report.results[:voltage_levels]
println("bank            : ", conn["transformer_banks"][1]["units"])
println("radial          : ", conn["is_radial"])
println("mixed-level bus : ", vl["mixed_phase_to_neutral_buses"])
split = only(f for f in report.findings if f.code == "I.PROV.SPLIT_PHASE_ZONE")
println("legs            : ", split.detail["legs"], ", high leg: ", split.detail["high_leg"])
bank            : ["lighting", "power"]
radial          : true
mixed-level bus : ["svc"]
legs            : ["a", "b"], high leg: ["c"]
  • The two units join the same two buses on different conductors. They are one bank: one branch of the network graph, not a loop.
  • Together they supply all three phase conductors, although neither does alone.
  • The bus has no single nominal voltage to neutral. Its level is reported as the leg voltage, and it is listed as a mixed-level bus.

The power flow agrees. Legs a and b solve at about 120 V and the high leg c at about √3 × 120 ≈ 208 V; the small shortfall is the voltage drop across the transformer impedances under load:

using JuMP, Ipopt

result = redirect_stdout(devnull) do
    solve_pf(net)
end
for t in ("a", "b", "c")
    v = result["bus"]["svc"][t]
    println(t, ": ", round(hypot(v["vr"], v["vi"]), digits = 1), " V to neutral")
end
a: 119.4 V to neutral
b: 119.4 V to neutral
c: 207.4 V to neutral

Japan: three-wire primary and V-connection

The Japanese 6.6 kV primary is three-wire, with no neutral conductor. A pole transformer connected between two phases feeds a 100/200 V single-phase three-wire service, and a V-connected pair feeds three-phase 200 V with 100 V lighting legs taken from the center-tapped unit.

net = case("jp_three_wire_v_connection")
report = summarize(net)
0 errors, 0 warnings
  zone at home  split_phase  phases ["a", "b"]
  zone at shop  split_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  zone at sub   three_phase  phases ["a", "b", "c"]
  bus levels (V): home=100  p1=3811  p2=3811  shop=100  sub=3811

The home is an ordinary split-phase zone at 100 V per leg. The shop is the same structure as the North American high-leg service, at Japanese voltages:

vl = report.results[:voltage_levels]
println("mixed-level bus: ", vl["mixed_phase_to_neutral_buses"],
        ", power unit: ", vl["high_leg_units"])
result = redirect_stdout(devnull) do
    solve_pf(net)
end
for t in ("a", "b", "c")
    v = result["bus"]["shop"][t]
    println(t, ": ", round(hypot(v["vr"], v["vi"]), digits = 1), " V to neutral")
end
mixed-level bus: ["shop"], power unit: ["pole_shop_power"]
a: 99.5 V to neutral
b: 99.6 V to neutral
c: 172.6 V to neutral

About 100 V on the two legs and about 173 V on the third conductor, again slightly below nominal under load.

What the source pins

A voltage_source fixes the voltage of each of its terminals to the reference. The 6.6 kV primary in this fixture is therefore referenced at the source, like a system earthed at the substation. A primary that is unearthed everywhere has to be modeled behind an isolating transformer, as in the next section.

An unearthed delta island

Industrial IT systems, and unearthed delta secondaries generally, have no intentional connection to earth. Two fixtures differ in one thing only: whether the cable's capacitance to earth is modeled.

bare = analyze(case("it_delta_island_unreferenced"))
cap = analyze(case("it_delta_island_capacitive"))
println("no capacitance modeled : ", [f.code for f in errors(bare)])
println("capacitance modeled    : ", [f.code for f in errors(cap)])
no capacitance modeled : ["E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE"]
capacitance modeled    : String[]

Without any path to earth, the voltages of the island to earth are not defined: line-to-line voltages are, but the whole triangle can sit anywhere. E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE reports exactly that, and it is a true error for a solver, which needs a unique answer. Cable capacitance is the real path to earth in such a system, and once it is in the data the island is referenced. The explanation page works through both.

What is recognized, and what is not

PracticeWhat the profiler reports
Three-phase four-wire, neutral earthed once or many timesthree_phase zones; earthing points counted; a neutral section with no earth is E.PROV.FLOATING_NEUTRAL
Neutral earthed through a resistor or arc-suppression coil at the transformer (r_neutral_*, x_neutral_*)a finite voltage reference; no error
Single-phase lateral on a three-phase feederpart of the feeder's zone
Center-tapped 120/240 V or 100/200 V servicesplit_phase zone, I.PROV.SPLIT_PHASE_ZONE
Banks of single-phase units: wye bank, open-wye/open-delta, V-connectionone branch (transformer_banks), supplying the union of the legs
High-leg (four-wire) deltasplit_phase zone with a high_leg; bus listed in mixed_phase_to_neutral_buses
Isolated SWERswer zone, I.PROV.SWER_ZONE, when a span carries one conductor and no neutral
Two-wire single-phase section behind a transformersingle_phase zone
Unearthed delta or IT islandreferenced only through modeled shunt admittance; otherwise E.INT.NO_VOLTAGE_REFERENCE
AC sections joined by a DC linkone connected network (dc_links)

Not recognized, and reported as such rather than guessed:

  • TN-S, TN-C-S, TT and IT as protective-earthing systems. The model has no protective-earth conductor, so the earthing report describes the represented paths to earth and marks the system type indeterminate.
  • Non-isolated SWER (a single-wire lateral tapped directly off a three-phase feeder). It shares the feeder's zone and is not tagged.
  • Corner-grounded delta. It can be written by perfectly grounding one phase terminal, which gives the island its reference, but no fixture or check is specific to it; read the zone and level output critically for such a case.
  • Two-phase loads as one WYE object. The specification fixes a WYE load at four terminals; write two SINGLE_PHASE loads instead.
  • Five-wire systems with a separate protective earth conductor. Device indexing is reported as unassessed for those buses.

If your network uses a practice that is not in either list, run it and read the zones and levels first: they show what the profiler understood.
