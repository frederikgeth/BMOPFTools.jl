Choose your tutorial

The sidebar follows the workflow from understanding network data to preparing, solving, and verifying a case. Advanced explanations, automation, and reference material provide separate routes. This page helps you choose by goal; the homepage learning map shows how the sections connect. Choose an advanced route for equation encodings, model extensions and numerical experiments.

If you are new, follow Installation & first steps, then First case: inspect, solve and verify. The first case uses a bundled synthetic feeder and needs no external data. You can stop after inspection if you do not need a solver yet.

The end-to-end tutorial from OpenDSS to a solved benchmark is a later, optional route. Its external dataset and license requirements are stated at the start of that page; its dataset examples are not executed by the docs build.

The judgment spine

Six of the tutorials, read in order, teach the reasoning a defensible study needs — not just how to call the tools, but when to trust the answer:

  1. Triage — Case study: findings triage at scale. A raw import arrives with dozens of findings; learn to separate defects (fix), judgment calls (decide and document), and disclosures (keep — they are the case's provenance record).
  2. Assumptions — From nameplate data to a defensible model. Incomplete datasheets in; a model out. What can be derived, what needs an assumption, and what must stay unknown — and the difference between standards-derived, standards-inspired, and synthetic values.
  3. Solve — First case and Developing OPF case families. Solve a supplied operating case, then compare explicit study scenarios.
  4. Verify — Trust but verify. Take one solved OPF and independently recompute KCL, voltage bounds, thermal loading, losses, and the objective — learning what a solver's LOCALLY_SOLVED status does and does not guarantee.
  5. Consequences — Impedance models & OPF decisions and Trusting the solver. How the modeling choices made in step 2 change the answer, and where the remaining traps live.
  6. Formulation — Custom formulations. When least-cost dispatch is not your question: hooks, replaced objectives, and multi-period coupling — with the discipline each rung demands.

Read top to bottom you get the missing arc: triage → model assumptions → solve → independent verification → fidelity consequences → your own formulation.

The modeling-choices track

Step 2's "assumptions" fan out into deep dives, one per modeling choice — each demonstrating live how that choice writes the answer:

  • Units, bases, scaling, and economics — the three unit systems every result rests on.
  • Impedance models & OPF decisions — network fidelity, symmetry, and what balanced models hide.
  • Choosing and identifying a load model — when constant-P/I/Z, ZIP, or exponential behavior is defensible.
  • From test report to transformer model — test data into validated fields, with the conventions made explicit.
  • Ground, neutral, and earth return — grounding assumptions, Kron reduction, and sequence coordinates.
