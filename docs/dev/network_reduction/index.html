Network reduction: which tool, and what it preserves

BMOPFTools offers three reductions. They remove different things, they are exact under different conditions, and they lose different information. This page explains what each one does, when it is exact, and how they combine. The numbers come from AC power flows on three feeders (the reproduction script is named at the end).

Two meanings of "Kron reduction"

Both operations below are called Kron reduction in the literature [Dörfler & Bullo 2013]. They share the algebra, a Schur complement, but apply it to different matrices under different conditions:

  • Conductor reduction works on a line's impedance matrix and imposes a zero voltage drop along the neutral of each line. The neutral may still carry current; the reduction is exact when the neutral voltage is zero at both ends of every line, that is, when the neutral is held at earth potential there. kron_reduce_neutrals classifies its result as "exact" only if no neutral had to be forced to earth and no constraint was dropped or converted.
  • Bus reduction works on the network's admittance matrix and imposes zero current injection at the eliminated nodes.

The first turns a four-wire model into a three-wire one with the same buses. The second keeps the conductors but removes buses. Elsewhere in these docs, for example in the methodology notes and the line-geometry tutorial, "Kron reduction" means the conductor version.

How they compare on real feeders

The error is the largest phase-to-neutral voltage-magnitude error over all covered bus-phases (kept and eliminated) and test loadings, relative to Ē. Opti-KRON uses the convex hull of the training samples. On the European LV feeder the 48 test loadings are the half-hour points of the 1,440 training minutes (minutes 15, 45, …); the Opti-KRON validation measures eliminated buses only on a different sample set, so its numbers differ.

What the table adds to the comparison above:

  • Coverage. Simplification is exact on these feeders, but merged buses no longer have a voltage. The European LV feeder is mostly long unloaded chains: simplification removes 87% of its buses and keeps a voltage for only 13% of bus-phases. On the load-dense feeder B it removes almost nothing. Opti-KRON keeps an estimate at every bus.
  • Finite MEN earthing. Neutral elimination is exact where the model earths every neutral perfectly (feeder B: 994 → 759 nodes, zero error). Feeder A earths its neutral through finite MEN electrodes; forcing it to ideal earth shifts phase-to-neutral voltages by about Ē, and no lift can undo that, because the neutral voltages are gone. (The European LV model is already three-wire, so the step changes nothing there.)
  • Composition. Simplifying first and letting Opti-KRON cluster the loaded buses gives 907 → 70 buses on the European LV feeder. Errors also compose: after neutral elimination on feeder A, Opti-KRON reaches 1.44 Ē in AC and the lift recovers only the Opti-KRON part. Opti-KRON alone reaches up to 1.04 Ē, because its certificate is for the linear model; leave a margin on Ē for the linear-versus-AC gap.

A recommended pipeline

  1. Run kron_reduce_neutrals only if every neutral is perfectly earthed in the model; check its _meta.kron_reduction provenance for "classification" => "exact". The scientific contract check_kron_boundary_recovery checks the same grounding precondition for a single source/target line pair with a declared recovery map; it is not a whole-network precheck.
  2. Run simplify_network if the voltages of unloaded pass-through buses need not be modeled.
  3. Run Opti-KRON on what remains, with an uncertainty set built from representative loadings, and keep opti_kron_required_buses so the result can be built. Opti-KRON is not yet linked to a PSK-* claim or exposed through the JSON execution interface.
  4. Solve the reduced case, then use lift_solution and an AC power flow on the original network to check the answer.

Reducing a feeder with a voltage guarantee introduces Opti-KRON on a six-bus feeder; Reducing a four-wire feeder, step by step walks through this pipeline on a generated feeder; and Error-bounded bus reduction has the mathematics.

Reproduction

The comparison table comes from scripts/reduction_comparison.jl (Gurobi for the reduction MILP, Ipopt for the power flows). The European LV data are the IEEE European Low Voltage Test Feeder (55 one-minute load profiles); the SAPN feeder rows use a non-public data set and cannot be reproduced from public data.
