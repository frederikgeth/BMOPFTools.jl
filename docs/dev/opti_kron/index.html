Error-bounded bus reduction (Opti-KRON)

Status

Not yet linked to a scientific-knowledge (PSK-*) claim or exposed through the JSON execution interface. Its behavior is tested and compared against the paper's published results (validation evidence).

Robust Opti-KRON [1] chooses which buses of a distribution network to keep so that the reduced network has as few buses as possible, while every original bus voltage stays within a threshold Ē of its estimate, for every loading in an uncertainty set. BMOPFTools implements the method and turns the result into a solvable BMOPF case.

For a hands-on introduction, start with the tutorial; for how it compares with the other reductions, see Network reduction.

The idea in one paragraph

Every bus j is assigned to a super-node i within H tree hops (A_ij = 1); super-nodes are kept, all other buses are eliminated. The devices of j move to i, phase by phase, and i's voltage stands in for j's. Moving a load changes voltages, so this has an error. Under a linear voltage model the error is linear in the injections, which makes "the error stays within Ē for every loading in a set" an exact, solvable constraint. With no injection left on them, the eliminated buses can then be removed exactly by Kron reduction [4].

Linear voltage model

Let u = [p; q] be the active and reactive power injections per node-phase (generation positive, in kW and kvar) and v the voltage magnitudes in p.u. of each node-phase's no-load magnitude U₀. BMOPFTools linearizes the full multiconductor network at its no-load operating point:

\[\Delta V = Y_{ff}^{-1}\,\Delta I, \qquad \Delta I_k = \frac{\overline{\Delta S_k}}{\overline{U_{0,k}}}, \qquad \Delta v_r = \frac{\operatorname{Re}\!\big(\overline{U_{0,r}}\,\Delta U_r\big)}{|U_{0,r}|^2},\]

where Yff is the passive admittance matrix ([`ybuspassive](@ref)) of all nodes not fixed by the source, and U is the phase-to-neutral voltage (or phase-to-earth where there is no ungrounded neutral). This gives v = 1 + K u with K = [K_P K_Q] ([optikronmodel`](@ref)). For a single-phase radial feeder with a flat no-load profile this is exactly LinDistFlow [2]: KP is the resistance shared by the two buses' paths to the source divided by U₀², KQ the shared reactance divided by U₀² (each scaled by 1000 for injections in kW and kvar). The general form also includes neutrals, MEN earthing, mutual coupling, transformers and meshes.

The error and the robust problem

When all devices of a cluster sit at its super-node i, the reduced voltages are ṽ = 1 + K A u. The error of bus j represented by super-node i is

\[e_{ij}(A, u) = \tilde v_i - v_j = c_{ij}(A)^\top u, \qquad c_{ij}(A)_k = K_{i,\,b(k)} - K_{j,k},\]

where b(k) is the super-node of node k. The coefficient c_ij(A) is affine in A. The robust reduction problem is

\[\begin{aligned} \min_A\ & \textstyle\sum_i A_{ii} \\ \text{s.t. }\ & \max_{u \in \mathcal U}\ c_{ij}(A)^\top u \le \bar E + M_{ij}(1 - A_{ij}) && \forall (i,j) \in \mathcal C \\ & \min_{u \in \mathcal U}\ c_{ij}(A)^\top u \ge -\bar E - M_{ij}(1 - A_{ij}) && \forall (i,j) \in \mathcal C \\ & \textstyle\sum_i A_{ij} = 1,\quad A_{ij} \le A_{ii},\quad A_{ss} = 1 && \text{(every bus has one super-node; slack kept)} \\ & A_{ij} \le A_{ik} \quad \forall k \in \mathcal P_{ij} && \text{(clusters are connected subtrees)} \end{aligned}\]

𝒞 holds the candidate pairs within H hops, and 𝒫_ij is the path between i and j. With the convex hull, the inner maxima are over finitely many samples and the problem is a mixed-integer linear program; with the ellipsoid and Wasserstein sets, its direct robust counterpart is not. BMOPFTools solves every set by constraint generation with MILP masters (below).

The box and the hull stay within the sampled range of each coordinate; the ellipsoid and the Wasserstein ball extrapolate beyond it. Passing only a few chosen samples to OptiKronHull gives the classical scenario-based Opti-KRON [3].

Solving: screening and adversarial constraint generation

opti_kron_screen removes candidate pairs that no feasible reduction can use and error constraints that always hold. Two tests are used: a worst-case voltage difference above 2Ē along a path (test 1), and interval bounds on the coefficients over the remaining candidates (test 2; box and hull only).

opti_kron_reduction then runs Algorithm 1 of [1]. A master MILP enforces the error bound only at a finite set of loadings 𝒰ᵗ. A closed-form adversary finds, for the master's assignment, the worst loading in 𝒰 for each bus, and adds those of the most-violated buses to 𝒰ᵗ (at most K per iteration, keyword K, default 3; not the sensitivity matrix K). Let Δ be the largest worst-case error over the set minus Ē (result.max_violation). The loop ends when Δ ≤ tol (default 10⁻³ Ē), or, when every proposed worst case is already enforced in the master, when Δ ≤ 10 tol (the residual is then solver feasibility tolerance). User-supplied initial loadings must lie in 𝒰, or the master would over-constrain the problem and its optimum would no longer be a lower bound; membership is checked exactly (an LP for the hull and the Wasserstein ℓ1 and ℓ∞ balls, a convex QP for the Wasserstein ℓ2 ball). The result is then certified for the whole set with error at most Ē + Δ. If the final master was solved to optimality (:optimal), it is also minimal among the assignments the master admits: candidate pairs within H hops that survive screening, connected clusters, the keep buses, and preserve_radial. The master's optimum (or its proven bound, after a time limit) is always a valid lower bound (result.lower_bound). A good MILP solver matters: on real feeders HiGHS often stalls, and Gurobi solves the same masters in seconds to minutes.

From an assignment to a solvable case

kron_reduce_buses builds the reduced BMOPF case:

  1. Relocate devices. Every load, generator and IBR of an eliminated bus is re-attached to its super-node with the same terminal labels. Devices stay separate objects, so their limits and costs survive.
  2. Eliminate exactly. The passive elements touching eliminated buses are Kron-reduced (Schur complement of their multiconductor admittance matrix) and written back as lines with inline series impedance and matrix shunts. Transformers and elements between kept buses are copied unchanged. For the AC model this step introduces no error: on a real four-wire feeder, the reduced case reproduces the full network with relocated devices to 5 × 10⁻¹³ p.u.
  3. Certify voltage limits (optional). If bus j is within ±Ē of its super-node i, then tightening i's band to $(U_j^{\min} + \bar E\,U_{0,j})\,U_{0,i}/U_{0,j}$ (and likewise for the maximum) keeps j within its own limits, within the certificate.

Two conditions apply. Transformer and source buses must be kept (opti_kron_required_buses), because a Kron equivalent across a transformer is not a BMOPF line. And with preserve_radial=true (the default), the reduced network stays radial: eliminating a bus that joins three or more directions containing super-nodes would couple them all (mesh fill-in). kron_reduce_buses checks fill-in on the physical graph, so it also catches fill-in through meshed edges that the MILP's tree-based constraint does not see. Current limits of eliminated lines have no exact equivalent; they are dropped and listed in the log.

The builder also refuses a few cases rather than silently approximating: time-varying impedances or limits on eliminated elements (the equivalent is one snapshot; reduce get_snapshot instead), and, with Ebar, a bus represented by the slack whose tightened band excludes the slack's fixed voltage (keep that bus). A kept neutral that reaches earth only through an eliminated bus keeps its ideal ground.

lift_solution maps a solution of the reduced case back to every original bus. The default linear lift uses the certificate itself: vj = ṽi − c_ij(A)ᵀu holds exactly in the linear model, and u (the solved device powers) is known after a solve. Only the second-order AC part of the error remains; in the Opti-KRON rows of the comparison on Network reduction this cut the error by about 10–200×.

Validation against the paper

On the IEEE European LV test feeder, the implementation reproduces the paper's candidate count (11,227 pairs) and structural maximum (88.6% of buses removed). For the scenario baseline, convex hull and box, the reductions at Ē = 0.001 agree with the paper to within 0.7 percentage points; the ellipsoid and Wasserstein results depend on calibration choices the paper leaves open. The full table, the AC check and the reproduction scripts are in Opti-KRON against the published results.

Differences from the paper

  • Linear model. Ybus-based sensitivities of the full multiconductor network instead of phase-coupled LinDistFlow; the two coincide for single-phase radial feeders.
  • Clusters do not cross transformers, except that the slack may represent buses within H hops across one, since its voltage is fixed.
  • Radiality. With preserve_radial=true (the default) the reduced network stays radial. The paper does not state this constraint, but its structural maximum is reproduced only with it.
  • Meshed networks use the breadth-first spanning tree from the source for hops, paths and radiality; the sensitivities come from the meshed Ybus.
  • Set calibration. The ellipsoid uses POET covariance [5] with an eigenvalue-ratio factor choice (at most five factors) and soft thresholding. The Wasserstein radius uses leave-one-out distances to the hull of the nearest other samples (radius=:hull): the paper's calibration, distances to the d = 0 part of 𝒰_W, is zero for every training sample whenever 1/(Nε) ≥ 1.
  • Only Algorithm 1 is implemented; passing all samples as initial with the convex hull reproduces the direct model. Screening test 2 covers the box and the convex hull only.
